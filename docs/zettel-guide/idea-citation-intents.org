#+TITLE: Idea: typed citation intents (supports / contradicts)
#+TYPE: idea
#+STATUS: brainstorm — depends on citations landing
#+DATE: 2026-07-05

* What it means

A raw citation count hides the most important fact about a claim: whether
the citing literature *supports* or *refutes* it. scite.ai classified
citation contexts at scale and found ~92.6% merely mention, 6.5% support,
0.8% contrast — the rare typed edges carry nearly all the signal.
Semantic Scholar's citation-intent work confirms the same: typed edges
beat untyped edges for scholarly graphs. scite needs an ML model to
recover intent post-hoc; an authoring tool can just *ask the author*, and
on Nostr the assertion is signed.

* Data

A nostrdown modifier on the cite reference:
={{cite:smith-2024|supports}}= / =|contradicts= / =|extends= / (default:
mention). Emitted as a tag on the *citing* event alongside the =cite=
resolution tag, e.g. =["cite", d-tag, "30161:pk:d-tag", relay, "supports"]=
(exact slot TBD with the citation spec). Author-asserted, per-edge,
verifiable.

* Representation (engine)

- Parse the modifier; carry intent through resolution.
- Aggregation query per cited target: "N supporting / M contradicting /
  K mentions known locally" — a backlink-family join
  ([[file:idea-backlinks.org][same machinery]]), filtered to cite edges,
  grouped by intent.

* UI/UX

- On any cited event's header/card: a small tally chip ("3 ⊕ / 1 ⊖"),
  expandable into the citing contexts (the sentence around each
  ={{cite:}}= — we have the content, we can show the passage).
- In compose: the reference builder offers the intent as a fourth field,
  defaulting to plain mention — zero friction when you don't care.

* Open questions

- Vocabulary: supports/contradicts/extends/mentions enough? (scite's
  taxonomy says yes; resist growth.)
- Should intent live on the 30161 citation event instead of the citing
  edge? (No — the same source can support one claim and contradict
  another; intent is a property of the *edge*.)
