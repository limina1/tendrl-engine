== The question

The coda claims: "Quality emerges not through gatekeeping but through
the natural selection of ideas that prove useful for further
thinking" ({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:aktaotwp-coda-the-streaming-edge-of-thought|Coda: The Streaming Edge of Thought}}). The emergent-governance kasten opens on
the same claim as a puzzle rather than an answer: given that Nostr is
"plausibly, the most adversarial social landscape that exists," the
one question that organizes everything is *how is there quality
discourse at all?* ({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:emergentgov-the-question|The question}}). The essay asserts
what the kasten sets out to explain.

== What the kasten already offers

The knowledge-commons literature replaces appropriation with
pollution and underprovision as the dilemmas of a nonsubtractive
resource (link:concept-idea-layer-nonsubtractive.adoc[idea layer]).
Its classical answer is Ostrom's fifth design principle, graduated
sanctions, which assumed a *unitary* arrangement: the institution
that monitors is linked to the one that sanctions
({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:concept-graduated-sanctions|graduated sanctions}}). The kasten's finding is
that Nostr splits the bundle: a kind-1984 report is a costly,
public, *non-binding* signal, and a different actor — a relay, a
client's web-of-trust scorer, a reader — decides what it means. In
the grammar of institutions the report is a norm with no named
"or-else" that nonetheless gets enforced, because the or-else is
crowdsourced across many potential enforcers
({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:claim-split-sanctions|split sanctions}};
{{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:concept-adico-grammar|adico grammar}}). The author's earlier paper laid
out the five-level ladder — follow lists, mutes, reports, relay
policy, network exclusion ({{ref:30040:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:governing-the-decentralized-commons|Governing the Decentralized Commons}}).

That *is* a mechanism for "natural selection of ideas" without a
gatekeeper: selection pressure is real but decoupled from any office.
The essay's "peer review happens continuously through the
participation of anyone competent to engage" is split sanctioning
described from the idea layer.

== What stays open

- *Selection signals for ideas, not conduct.* The kasten's coding
  table maps kinds to institutional statements for governance
  (reports, mutes, policies, NIPs). The essay needs the positive
  half: which events select an idea *up*? Highlights (kind 9802),
  citations (`a` and `q` tags), forks of an index, bookshelf
  inclusion (kind 30045), insertions and their promotion. A table of
  those is the coding instrument for anarchistic knowledge.
- *Legitimacy.* The kasten's anomaly — reports from high-centrality
  accounts move relay filters while peripheral reports do not — has
  an idea-layer twin the essay would dislike: do highlights from
  central accounts select ideas more than peripheral ones? If so,
  "guardianship without office" is still guardianship
  ({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:concept-guardianship-without-office|guardianship without office}}).
- *Decoupled vs unitary.* Whether decoupled sanctioning governs
  better than unitary enforcement is, in the kasten's words, "the
  most self-contained publishable question in the program"
  ({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:q-decoupled-vs-unitary-sanctions|Q: decoupled vs unitary sanctions}}). For the essay it is
  the difference between its optimism and a finding.
