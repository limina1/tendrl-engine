== The idea

The essay's title phrase has a named ancestor. Paul Feyerabend's
_Against Method_ (1975) carries the subtitle _Outline of an
Anarchistic Theory of Knowledge_, and its thesis — that there is no
single scientific method, that every methodological rule has been
productively violated at some decisive moment, and that the only
principle that survives every case is "anything goes" — is the
essay's "intelligence in its natural state" made into an argument
from the history of science (link:src-feyerabend-against-method.adoc[source note]).
Feyerabend's Galileo used rhetoric, propaganda and "epistemological
tricks"; the essay's anarchistic mind wanders and cross-pollinates.
Both hold that the productive move was the unauthorized one.

The essay's insistence that anarchistic knowledge "is not
anti-intellectual — it's differently intellectual" is also
Feyerabend's own qualification: epistemological anarchism was never
a claim that nothing is known, only that no rule set determines in
advance how knowing must proceed. And Feyerabend's *proliferation*
principle — keep many incompatible theories alive because each
exposes facts the others hide — is requisite variety for a science
(link:concept-wandering-as-requisite-variety.adoc[requisite variety]).

== The moderate cousins

Two less anarchic relatives fill in the mechanism the essay only
asserts. Michael Polanyi's "The Republic of Science" (_Minerva_,
1962) argued that science is a spontaneous order: independent
researchers coordinate by *mutual adjustment*, each fitting their
work to what the others have found, with no central plan and no
authority able to direct the whole. Hayek's "The Use of Knowledge in
Society" (_AER_ 35, 1945) supplied the premise — the knowledge that
matters is dispersed, local, and unavailable to any centre. Polanyi's
republic is the essay's "collective intelligence" with an economics
attached, and it is the direct ancestor of the Ostroms'
polycentricity: many formally independent centres, taking each other
into account, under an overarching set of rules
({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:concept-polycentricity|polycentricity}}).

== Where the essay goes further, and Ostrom pulls it back

Feyerabend and Polanyi are describing science, a commons with strong
boundary rules and unitary sanctions (peer review, retraction,
reputation). The essay extends the argument to the mechanic, the
gardener and the bartender — knowledge under default conditions,
with no boundary rule at all. That extension is where Ostrom is
needed rather than Feyerabend: the commons school's mature statement
defines a commons as a *mode of governance*, institutionalized
sharing among members of a community, not as an absence of rules
({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:concept-gkc-framework|gkc framework}}). "Anything goes" is a claim
about method, not about facilities. The essay is right that
knowledge escapes; the emergent-governance kasten is what explains
how the escaped knowledge still has quality discourse at all.
