== Cybernetics: the Macy Conferences, 1946–1953

Ten meetings in New York under the Josiah Macy Jr. Foundation, whose
programme aimed at interdisciplinary approaches to the social,
behavioural and medical sciences. Co-organized by the
neuropsychiatrist Warren McCulloch and the foundation's medical
director Frank Fremont-Smith. The heading was "Cybernetics: Circular
Causal and Feedback Mechanisms in Biological and Social Systems," and
the invited core mixed mathematics (Wiener, von Neumann, Pitts),
engineering, neurophysiology (McCulloch, Rosenblueth), anthropology
(Bateson, Mead), psychology, psychiatry, linguistics and sociology.
Ross Ashby attended in the early 1950s. The transactions were the
first place "information" and "feedback" were worked into one theory
"equally applicable to living beings and machines, economic and
cognitive processes." Historians of science call it one of the first
organized approaches to transdisciplinarity.

The essay's sentence — "biologists, engineers, and mathematicians
who weren't supposed to be talking to each other" — is accurate on
the disciplines and understates the organization: a patron, a
convenor, a stable membership, a theme. [[Cybernetics]] was
promiscuous in content and bounded in form
(link:concept-facilitated-promiscuity.adoc[facilitated promiscuity]).

== Complexity: the Santa Fe Institute, 1984, and the 1987 workshop

Founded in 1984 by George Cowan with Murray Gell-Mann, David Pines,
Philip Anderson, Nick Metropolis and others from Los Alamos, with the
stated purpose of countering "the increasing specialization the
founders observed in science" by synthesis across disciplines. The
essay's "physicists started thinking like ecologists and economists
began borrowing from biology" has a precise date: the September 1987
workshop "Evolutionary Paths of the Global Economy," convened by the
physicists Anderson and Pines with the economist Kenneth Arrow, which
brought economists together with physical and biological scientists.
Its proceedings, _The Economy as an Evolving Complex System_ (1988),
include W. Brian Arthur's "Self-Reinforcing Mechanisms in Economics,"
and the workshop seeded the Institute's first research programme.
[[Complex system]] science as a field dates from that composition.

== The link to the Ostroms

Lewis & Aligica (2024) document that Vincent Ostrom absorbed
Ashby's requisite variety and hierarchy of feedback at Stanford in
1955–56, and that the three levels of rules are "an expression of
the underlying logic of the very phenomenon of self-organization"
per cybernetics
({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:src-ostroms-cybernetics|Lewis & Aligica 2024 — the Ostroms' cybernetics}}).
So the framework behind the emergent-governance kasten descends from
the first of the essay's two examples.
