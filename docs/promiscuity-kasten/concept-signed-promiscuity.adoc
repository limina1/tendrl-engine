== The idea

The essay's coda pushes anarchistic knowledge to a limit where
"authorship dissolves": "You can't copyright a river. You can't
patent the moment when two currents converge." Nostr, which the
essay names as the base for an unenclosable commons, does the
opposite with every drop — each event is signed, timestamped, and
nameable by hash. The apparent contradiction dissolves once
*attribution* and *ownership* are separated, and the
property-rights literature the Ostroms used is the instrument for
separating them.

Schlager and Ostrom's bundle distinguishes access, withdrawal,
management, exclusion, and alienation. The emergent-governance
kasten reads Nostr through it: a relay operator holds a proprietor's
bundle over the facility (management and exclusion) but "no
alienation over anyone's identity, because keys and follow graphs
leave with the user" ({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:concept-property-rights-bundles|property rights bundles}}).
And no one holds *withdrawal* over the pool — deletion is advisory,
broadcast to many facilities is LOCKSS by default, so the "fugitive
artifact" Hess and Ostrom worried about in 2003 cannot occur
({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:concept-ideas-artifacts-facilities|ideas artifacts facilities}}).

So a signature on Nostr confers attribution and nothing else. It
does not let the author withdraw the idea, does not let them exclude
a reader, does not let them stop a fork. This is exactly the
condition the coda asks for: "every thought carries traces of its
entire history," and "the reader's response becomes as important as
the author's original insight." Signed promiscuity is provenance
without enclosure — a river whose drops know where they came from.

== The essay's own instance

NKBIP-01 publications, the format this essay is published in, make
the point structurally. An index is a list of pointers to sections
any of which may be by another author; a section can appear in many
indexes; a fork is a new index under a new key that re-points at the
same signed sections. This note is itself an *insertion*: signed by
an assistant's key, anchored to the author's section, carrying a
pointer to the author's request, promotable into the essay by the
author and never re-authored. The essay wanted "interfaces that
allow multiple people to contribute to ongoing streams of thought
without requiring final consensus or closure." Anchored, attributed,
promotable notes are one such interface, and they only work because
attribution was kept while ownership was dropped.

== Where it strains

Attribution has a cost the essay does not weigh: the signed record
is effectively irrevocable, and the right to be forgotten strains
against it ({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:q-advisory-deletion|Q: advisory deletion}}). The streaming
researcher whose "original hypothesis becomes unrecognizable" also
cannot retract the recognizable version. Anarchistic knowledge on a
signed substrate is more honest about lineage than the essay's river
and less forgiving than it.
