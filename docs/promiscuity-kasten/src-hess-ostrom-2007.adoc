== What they are

The founding texts of the knowledge-commons school, by [[Elinor
Ostrom]] and the librarian-scholar Charlotte Hess. The 2003 article
introduces the three-way decomposition — *ideas*, *artifacts*,
*facilities* — in place of the natural-resource pair (resource
system / resource units), and reads the digital library through the
property-rights bundle. The 2007 volume collects the field's first
survey, with the editors' introduction and their chapter 3, "A
Framework for Analyzing the Knowledge Commons," which imports the
decomposition as the IAD's biophysical layer. The 2007 introduction
contains the "look what is happening!" passage the
emergent-governance kasten treats as the founders' stated limit.

== Claims this annotation uses

- Ideas are nonrivalrous; "the more people who share useful
  knowledge, the greater the common good" (2007 intro, p. 5)
  → link:concept-idea-layer-nonsubtractive.adoc[the idea layer].
- Commons ≠ open access ≠ public domain; "a commons is not value
  laden" (2007 intro, pp. 13–14)
  → link:claim-anarchistic-is-not-open-access.adoc[not open access].
- Enclosure: "New technologies can enable the capture of what were
  once free and open public goods" (2007 intro, p. 10) → the
  essay's _Institutional Capture_ section.
- Withdrawal rights: licensed artifacts are "fugitive," publishers
  "have the right of withdrawal" (2003, p. 134); the remedy was
  LOCKSS → link:concept-signed-promiscuity.adoc[signed promiscuity].
- "Being Prepared for Change": fixed rules place "too much
  confidence in the current state of knowledge"; systems allowing
  change are "suboptimal in the short run but wiser in the long run"
  (2007 ch. 3) → link:claim-enclosure-release-is-expand-contract.adoc[expand–contract].
