== The idea

Ashby's law of requisite variety: a regulator can only absorb the
disturbances it has moves to match — "there must exist as much variety
in the strategies available as there is variety in the conditions
that obtain." Vincent Ostrom took the law directly from Ashby at
Stanford in 1955–56 and called Ashby's theory of adaptive behaviour
"a basic foundation of my own work"; Elinor restated it in 1995 for
governance systems (Lewis & Aligica 2024, pp. 5–7;
{{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:src-ostroms-cybernetics|Lewis & Aligica 2024 — the Ostroms' cybernetics}};
{{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:concept-requisite-variety|requisite variety}}).

The essay's second subsection, "The Fertility of Intellectual
Wandering," is Ashby's law stated for a single mind. "The world itself
is interconnected in ways that our institutional divisions obscure"
— the environment's variety does not respect disciplinary lines. A
mind confined to one department has fewer moves than the world has
disturbances; the person "who notices similarities between jazz
improvisation and software debugging isn't being distracted — they're
discovering patterns that more focused minds might miss." Wandering
is the regulator acquiring variety.

== The parallel discipline

The emergent-governance kasten makes a methodological choice from the
same law: its catalogue of exclusion methods is "deliberately left
unfinished," because a complete catalogue "would be evidence the
system had *stopped* matching its environment." Ostrom herself
counted 27+ boundary rules in use and concluded that "no analyst can
perform a complete analysis" of their combinations (_Digging Deeper_,
pp. 90–91; {{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:src-digging-deeper|Ostrom — Digging Deeper}}). The essay reaches the same
place from the other side: "anarchistic knowledge" is knowledge that
refuses to be enumerated by its institutions, and the refusal is
functional, not undisciplined.

This gives the essay a sharper defence than it offers itself. The
academic mind "would call this undisciplined"; the reply is not that
discipline is bad but that a knower with less variety than its world
cannot regulate it. Requisite variety turns "the anarchistic mind
recognizes it as intelligence in its natural state" from an
assertion into a theorem with a named author.

== Where it strains

Ashby's regulator is one system with many moves; the essay's wanderer
is one mind. But the emergent-governance kasten notes that Nostr's
variety is distributed across *uncoordinated actors* — variety of
regulators, not just of moves. The essay's third section, "The
Ecology of Conversation," is where its subject shifts from the
wandering mind to the wandering conversation, and that is where the
Nostr case actually lives. The open question the kasten keeps —
whether robustness is generativity rather than redundancy
({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:q-generativity-robustness|Q: generativity robustness}}) — is the essay's "positive
feedback loops that generate increasing abundance" put in a testable
form.
