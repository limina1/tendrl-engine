== The idea

The essay's two examples of "unauthorized intersections" — cybernetics
emerging "from conversations between biologists, engineers, and
mathematicians who weren't supposed to be talking to each other," and
complexity theory developing "when physicists started thinking like
ecologists and economists began borrowing from biology" — are
historically accurate and instructively *not* anarchic in
organization. Both were facilitated.

Cybernetics took shape at the Macy Conferences, ten meetings in New
York between 1946 and 1953, sponsored by a foundation, co-organized
by Warren McCulloch and Frank Fremont-Smith, with an invited and
roughly stable core (Wiener, von Neumann, Bateson, Mead, Pitts,
Rosenblueth) and a standing title: "Circular Causal and Feedback
Mechanisms in Biological and Social Systems"
(link:src-macy-and-santa-fe.adoc[source note]). Complexity theory's
institutional home was the Santa Fe Institute, founded in 1984 by Los
Alamos physicists precisely "as an alternative to the increasing
specialization" they saw; the physicists-meet-economists moment the
essay describes was a specific September 1987 workshop convened by
Anderson, Arrow and Pines, whose proceedings became _The Economy as
an Evolving Complex System_.

In both cases the promiscuity was real — the disciplines did not
authorize it — but it was hosted by a *bounded institution composed
on the open commons of science*: a venue with a membership rule, a
conduct rule ("circular causality" as the admission ticket), a
patron paying the maintenance cost, and free exit back into one's
home field. That is the curated middle of the relay spectrum
({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:claim-relay-spectrum|relay spectrum}};
{{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:claim-composable-bounded-institutions|composable bounded institutions}}), and it is the
form Brian Eno named *scenius* — "the intelligence and intuition of a
whole cultural scene," communal rather than individual genius
(link:src-creativity-and-commoning-literature.adoc[source note]).

== The loop back to Ostrom

One further fact makes this note load-bearing for the whole kasten.
Ross Ashby attended the Macy circle, and it was Ashby whom Vincent
Ostrom met at Stanford in 1955–56 and from whom the Ostroms took
requisite variety, the hierarchy of feedback, and the rationale for
levels of rules (Lewis & Aligica 2024;
{{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:src-ostroms-cybernetics|Lewis & Aligica 2024 — the Ostroms' cybernetics}}). The institutional-analysis
framework the author uses to read Nostr is itself a product of the
cross-pollination episode the essay cites. The essay's example
proves the essay's thesis and *also* produced the theory that
governs this annotation.

== Where it strains

Polycentricity in the Ostroms' sense expects the centres to interact
and check one another; the emergent-governance kasten notes that
Nostr's centres "mostly never meet," with the protocol substituting
for negotiated coordination
({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:concept-polycentricity|polycentricity}}). Macy and Santa Fe were the
opposite: rooms where the centres met face to face. The essay's
streaming coda — thought that moves "at the speed of connection
itself" — is the Nostr limit; the historical examples are the
slow-glacier limit it also names. Authority inside such scenes takes
the form the kasten calls guardianship without office — McCulloch and
Gell-Mann led by observable contribution, not position
({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:concept-guardianship-without-office|guardianship without office}}).
