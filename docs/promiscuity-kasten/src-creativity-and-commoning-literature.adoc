Five bodies of work the essay's opening section draws on without
naming. Each is given with what it adds and where it sits relative to
Ostrom.

== Koestler — bisociation (The Act of Creation, 1964)

Creative acts share one pattern, *bisociation*: an element from one
"matrix of thought" is perceived simultaneously in a second,
previously unrelated matrix. Humour, discovery and art are the same
operation at different emotional pitches. The essay's "poetry
infiltrates physics when you need metaphors" and "jazz improvisation
and software debugging" are bisociations. Koestler also notes that
when two matrices have ripened, multiple simultaneous discoveries
follow — the essay's "ideas arrive like uninvited guests" at a
population level.

== Polanyi — the republic of science (Minerva, 1962); Hayek — dispersed knowledge (AER, 1945)

[[Michael Polanyi]]: science coordinates by *mutual adjustment* of
independent initiatives, a spontaneous order no authority could plan
without destroying. Hayek supplied the premise that the relevant
knowledge is dispersed and local. Both are direct ancestors of the
Ostroms' polycentricity — many centres, mutual adjustment, an
overarching rule set — and of the essay's "collective intelligence"
(link:concept-epistemological-anarchism.adoc[epistemological anarchism]).

== Eno and Kelly — scenius (Kevin Kelly, "Scenius, or Communal Genius," 2008)

Brian Eno's coinage: "the intelligence and the intuition of a whole
cultural scene … the communal form of the concept of the genius."
Kelly's essay lists what nurtures it — mutual appreciation that
applauds risky moves, rapid exchange of tools and techniques, network
effects of success, local tolerance for the novelties. That list is
a set of *rules-in-use* for a bounded scene on an open commons, which
is why the Macy and Santa Fe episodes read as scenius
(link:concept-facilitated-promiscuity.adoc[facilitated promiscuity]).
The author's own wiki transcript uses the term for the economy at
scale (link:src-authors-adjacent-publications.adoc[adjacent publications]).

== Benkler — commons-based peer production ("Coase's Penguin," Yale Law Journal 112, 2002)

A third mode of production beside markets and firms, with
"systematic advantages" when the object is information and the
capital is widely distributed. The essay's "open source projects
that demonstrate new forms of collaborative creation" and "wikis that
show how collective editing can produce higher quality content" are
Benkler's examples; his explanation (modularity, granularity, cheap
integration) is the mechanism the essay's "lower barriers to
participation … make contributions visible" gestures at.

== Bollier & Helfrich — commoning (Free, Fair, and Alive, 2019)

Commons as a *verb*: not a resource but "dynamic, living social
processes." This is the essay's register — "knowledge is most alive
when it's most free" — inside the commons literature, and it is the
wing the exchange-theory critics call romantic. The
emergent-governance kasten deliberately keeps out of that corner by
making its contribution an observing instrument rather than an
endorsement
({{ref:30041:dc4cd086cd7ce5b1832adf4fdd1211289880d2c7e295bcb0e684c01acee77c06:syn-emergent-governance-framework|the synthesis: a framework for emergent governance}}).
The essay, if it wants to be read alongside the kasten, has the same
choice to make.
