The mechanism behind the closer, with the medical knowledge commons as
its standing instance. The two properties that normally trade off are
held simultaneously:

- *Institutional quality control as a centralized function.* A
  gate-kept community (e.g. a medical knowledge commons) runs its own
  relay as a bounded institution — curation, membership, maintenance,
  strict write policy. On its own relay, outsiders read but do not
  write ("read-only on their relay").
- *No global excludability.* The same signed events are freely
  readable and commentable on every other relay. Globally, no one can
  be excluded from the *conversation* — only from a *venue*. The
  community's ideas circulate and take comment everywhere; the
  community's canonical surface stays curated.

So the decentralized non-bannability is not in tension with
institutional quality control: it is what makes the gated institution
safe to build (its members were never at risk of losing the commons)
and honest to run (its claims are contestable outside its gate).
This is why the medical-commons application matters to the governance
paper at all — it is what gets *built* once the substrate is proven —
while remaining the other paper's subject (see
link:mission.adoc[mission] on the two-paper separation).
