== The idea

Rules nest: _operational_ rules govern day-to-day action;
_collective-choice_ rules determine who may change operational rules
and how; _constitutional-choice_ rules determine who crafts
collective-choice rules; a rarely analyzed _metaconstitutional_ level
(cultural constraints on what counts as legitimate) underlies all
(Ostrom 2007, pp. 44–45; McGinnis pp. 8–9). Two consequences: changes
at one level occur within a "fixed" deeper level, and deeper rules are
costlier to change, which stabilizes expectations (Ostrom p. 44).
Collective-choice activity = prescribing, invoking, monitoring,
applying, enforcing rules (p. 46). Level shifts happen inside ordinary
interaction without notice — the boss/employee "change the way we do X"
example (p. 33). McGinnis adds the _level-shifting strategy_: actors
ill-served in one arena move the question to an adjacent level,
"also known as forum-shopping" (p. 9).

== The Nostr engagement

Mapping: operational = posting, muting, reporting, filtering;
collective-choice = relay policy changes, client defaults, allowlist
edits, NIP adoption by implementations; constitutional = the NIP
process and protocol core; metaconstitutional ≈ cryptography and
key-ownership norms, changeable in principle but treated as fixed
(link:concept-governance-as-software.adoc[concept-governance-as-software]). Nostr's distinctive property: all
three levels emit the same observable substance (events, commits,
config), so level shifts that Ostrom says pass unnoticed leave a
timestamped trail — link:claim-formation-observable.adoc[claim-formation-observable]. Forum-shopping is
institutionalized as costless relay exit: link:concept-exit-voice.adoc[concept-exit-voice],
link:concept-interjurisdictional-competition.adoc[concept-interjurisdictional-competition]. Note the strain: most users
never touch a relay's collective-choice arena (Ostrom's p. 32 condition
for escaping open-access tragedy), yet governance still works —
link:q-does-competition-improve-governance.adoc[q-does-competition-improve-governance].
