:title: Q: How does a commons reconstitute boundaries it cannot enforce?
:type: question
:status: open
:drafted-by: assistant
:tags: ostrom-extension, boundary, sybil

Ostrom extension #1. Design principle 1 assumes a
definable "who belongs" boundary; on Nostr identity-layer enforcement
is impossible (free pseudonymous keys, unlimited entry). Yet
boundaries exist — redundantly: the Sybil that beats the social layer
hits the relay filter; the one that beats the filter scores ~0 in a
client web of trust; different users run different combinations, so
there is *no single global boundary to defeat*.

Open questions: Is redundant, heterogeneous boundary-reconstitution a
stable substitute for enforceable membership? What does "clearly
defined boundaries" mean when the principle must be re-specified as
*boundary-setting capacity* rather than a boundary
(link:src-ukac-introduction.adoc[src-ukac-introduction])? Where
does IAD put a participant who cannot be excluded from the situation
at all (link:agent-iad-core.adoc[agent-iad-core] flag)?
