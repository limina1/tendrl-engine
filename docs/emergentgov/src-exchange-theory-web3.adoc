== What it is

Buchanan's "politics without romance" transposed to web3: the
"romantic view" (community voting, town halls, public-spirited
consensus) obscures governance as "an entrepreneurial process of
bargaining and exchange" under uncertainty (659–661). Three cases —
Curve Wars vote markets, Lido, Metagovernance Project — plus a
robustness / anti-Nirvana-fallacy payoff section.

== Core claims

1. No general theory of web3 governance; the field is "mostly an
   experimental science" (660).
2. Public-interest deliberation "only serves to obscure the
   underlying structure of complex exchanges at play" (662).
3. CAUTION: Ostrom/IAD/GKC-style DAO analysis is filed in the
   romantic camp — such analyses "draw heavily on Ostrom's (1990)
   'governing the commons' model" (661), citing the Bodon et al.
   companion of Source 3's authors.
4. Forking is a standing constitutional mechanism: the "calculus of
   consent motivating exit," amplified by composability (663).
5. Governance exists because of "contractual incompleteness (in
   social and smart contracts)"; governance innovation is itself
   value creation via entrepreneurial discovery (663–664). Vote
   trading/quadratic/bond voting can be Pareto-improving
   intensity revelation (664).
6. Pseudonymity and composability are the two infrastructure
   features driving governance adaptation (665).
7. Curve Wars: layered vote markets "injected more local knowledge
   into the collective governance process" (665–667).
8. Lido became a dominant Ethereum-governance stakeholder "even
   though the formal institutions of the underlying protocol have
   not changed" (667–668). Metagov: orgs that "lower the transaction
   costs of political bargaining and exchange" (668–669).
9. Layering "pushes in the direction of further polycentricity and
   robustness"; reading governance innovation as attack vectors is
   "the 'Nirvana fallacy' (Demsetz, 1967) applied to blockchain
   governance" (669).
10. Anti-design conclusion: limits to ex ante design; beware
    "designed simplistic governance systems over more complex
    emergent governance systems" (670).

== As a format model

Paradigm-contrast template: (1) name and genealogize the incumbent
frame ("romantic"); (2) install the rival (exchange); (3) definitions
separating object levels (governance of/by); (4) mechanism section on
what only the new frame predicts; (5) three short cases, each
captioned visible-only-through-the-new-lens; (6) normative robustness
payoff; (7) implications + research program. Imitable: pose designed
platform moderation / design-principle retrodiction as incumbent,
observed emergent exchange of moderation signals on Nostr as rival,
then three mini-cases (report cascade, relay policy change, bounded
medical commons).

- TRANSFERS: emergent permissionless discovery as the object of
  study; "build them and empirically observe what happens" (660) —
  methods quote for link:claim-formation-observable.adoc[claim-formation-observable].
- TRANSFERS: Lido logic — power composed on an unchanged substrate
  (667) — template for link:claim-composable-bounded-institutions.adoc[claim-composable-bounded-institutions].

- BREAKS: the exchange runs through priced, transferable governance

== Tensions / open questions

- Cases are web-source narratives, not systematic data — our
  event-data advantage.
- Tautology risk: any outcome redescribable as discovered value; no
  falsification criterion.
- Plutocracy answered only by asserted layering-robustness (669) —
  link:q-does-competition-improve-governance.adoc[q-does-competition-improve-governance].
- Celebrates the Lido concentration Source 1 treats as risk; admits
  poor DAO voter engagement (665) yet stays optimistic.
