:title: Formation is observable for the first time
:type: claim
:status: drafted
:drafted-by: assistant
:tags: hinge, survivorship, formation, empirics

The hinge of the whole program. Ostrom's eight design principles were
read off commons that had *already survived* — survivorship-filtered.
Formation itself was never observed; the field reverse-engineers rules
from successful institutions after the fact. Nostr is the first commons
you can *watch govern itself into existence*: every report (kind 1984),
mute, follow-edit, and relay-policy change is a signed, timestamped,
typed event.

This answers Hess & Ostrom on their own terms — two verbatim quotes
(locators in link:src-analyzing-knowledge-commons.adoc[src-analyzing-knowledge-commons]):

1. The limitation: "Thus far we (all of us!) have mainly been at the
   'look what is happening!' stage."
2. The prerequisite: "After more efforts succeed and others fail, we
   will be able to better understand what makes various knowledge
   commons work." — principles can only be extracted by watching
   commons succeed *and fail*.

So the claim is not "look, a cool decentralized platform" (the named
failure mode) — it is the empirical condition their own conclusion
calls for. Watching in real time also means the *failures* are in the
record, which the survivorship-filtered method structurally lacked.
