== What it is

A conceptual proposal (explicitly "a prologue," no prototype) for a
composable governance layer for online communities, grounded in
Ostrom's IAD, with an ontology (Platform/Instance/Org/Module/Entity/
Monitor/Permission) and an open-standard implementation strategy.
First author is Schneider (not Zhang/Frey); PolicyKit appears nowhere
in the paper — do not cite it to this source.

== Core claims

1. 16:1 — governance tools are "remarkably impoverished, typically
   relying on assigning unchecked power to admins and moderators."
2. 16:2 — "Monarchic or oligarchic practices emerge in online networks
   more easily and frequently than liberal or democratic ones."
3. 16:2 — four design goals: Modularity, Expressiveness, Portability,
   Interoperability.
4. 16:2, 16:15–16:16 — chosen strategy (over cloud API and DLT): "the
   most flexible strategy would be to define an open standard...
   implemented through a variety of open-source software libraries."
5. 16:4 — decentralized/federated platforms "favor 'exit' over
   'voice'" (Hirschman).
6. 16:5–16:7 — IAD chosen for its empiricism toward not-yet-theorized
   forms; communities formalize action situations threaded "across
   polycentric networks" (16:7).
7. 16:10–16:11 — Monitors: read-only composable evaluators of
   governance data, e.g. cross-instance censorship comparisons.
8. 16:11 — "all permissions... derive from those that the platform
   administrators specify at the level of the Instance."
9. 16:16–16:17 — digital-juries use case: flag → jury → removal as one
   binding platform pipeline.
10. 16:18 — computational governance data enables "new and more useful
    taxonomies of governance practices."
11. 16:19–16:20 — limitations: expressible space undefined; false
    universalism risk; software insufficient without a "civic sphere"
    ("the confluence of tools, culture, and context").

== Hooks to the Nostr thesis

- CENTRAL: Nostr is a de-facto realization of their chosen strategy —
  an open standard (NIPs) implemented by independent libraries, clients,
  relays — but grown, not designed as a governance layer; governance
  accreted as NIPs (kind-1984 reports, mute lists, NIP-65). The thing
  they wanted, grown instead of designed. Yet Nostr breaks their
  ontology: no Platform/Instance root (contra 16:11 permissions
  derivation), permissions polycentric all the way down, and
  interoperability default-on where theirs is consent-gated
  (16:11–16:12) — link:claim-no-global-bannability.adoc[claim-no-global-bannability], link:concept-polycentricity.adoc[concept-polycentricity].
- Design-goals audit: modularity ` event kinds; expressiveness ` any
  kind mintable; portability = signed events readable everywhere;
  interoperability = protocol-native — link:claim-multiplicity-stack.adoc[claim-multiplicity-stack].
- Monitors (16:10–16:11) exist today as WoT scorers, kind-1984
  dashboards, relay-policy trackers — link:claim-formation-observable.adoc[claim-formation-observable].
- Digital juries decoupled: on Nostr flag, judgment, and enforcement
  are separate uncoordinated actors — link:claim-split-sanctions.adoc[claim-split-sanctions],
  link:q-decoupled-vs-unitary-sanctions.adoc[q-decoupled-vs-unitary-sanctions].
- Exit-over-voice (16:4): Nostr makes exit near-frictionless (portable
  keys and graph), turning exit into operator discipline —
  link:q-does-competition-improve-governance.adoc[q-does-competition-improve-governance], link:concept-interjurisdictional-competition.adoc[concept-interjurisdictional-competition].
- Civic-sphere caveat (16:20): event streams miss the GitHub/Telegram
  culture layer — pairs with Schroeder's off-platform worry (1956).

== Tensions / open questions

- Aspiration, not spec: no benchmarks to borrow; strengthens the
  "unplanned fulfillment" framing but proves nothing empirical.
- Top-down residue (16:8, 16:11) sits closer to platform-hosted
  governance than Nostr's operator-less substrate.
- DLT rejected for expressiveness costs (16:15); Nostr is the unnamed
  third path: open standard + cryptographic identity, no ledger.
- No adversarial model (spam, sybils) — exactly where Nostr's record
  is richest — link:q-boundary-sybil.adoc[q-boundary-sybil].
- Ostrom nesting assumes containment (16:9); Nostr's bounded
  institutions overlap and cross-cut —
  link:claim-composable-bounded-institutions.adoc[claim-composable-bounded-institutions].
