== What it is

Ostrom's own mature summary of the IAD framework: the challenges of
studying institutions, the framework/theory/model distinction, the
action arena and its seven-element action situation, the three exogenous
variable clusters (rules-in-use, biophysical world, community), the
seven rule types, three-plus levels of rules, evaluative criteria, and a
survey of empirical applications (police services, CPR databases, IFRI).

== Core claims

1. p. 23 — institutions are shared concepts (rules, norms, strategies),
   not organizations; and they are invisible: study rules-in-use, "the
   dos and don'ts that one learns on the ground."
2. pp. 24–25 — rules are configural, not additive: one rule's effect
   depends on which other rules are in force.
3. pp. 25–26 — frameworks identify universal elements; theories make
   working assumptions; models fix parameters. Many theories per
   framework, many models per theory.
4. pp. 28–29 — action arena = action situation (7 variable clusters) +
   actors (4 clusters).
5. pp. 30–31 — Homo economicus is substitutable; for CPR settings assume
   fallible learners, "intendedly rational but only limitedly so."
6. p. 32 — open access predicts "a race to use up the resource" — unless
   appropriators share access to collective-choice arenas.
7. p. 33 — "agreement about the legitimacy of boundaries and reliable
   monitoring" beats formal ownership as predictor of cooperation.
8. p. 33 — level shifts pass unnoticed in ordinary interaction (the
   boss–employee "change the way we do X" example).
9. p. 37 — "[R]ules are not self-formulating, self-determining, or
   self-enforcing"; rules inherit language's ambiguity.
10. p. 38 — classify rules by their impact on action-situation elements:
    exactly seven types.
11. pp. 44–45 — all rules nest in deeper rules governing their change;
    deeper rules are costlier to change, stabilizing expectations.
12. p. 44 — under-studied: "rule-ordered competition among two or more
    potential producers of public goods and services."
13. p. 48 — externally imposed sanction systems "crowd the benefits of a
    self-imposed set of sanctions"; measured responses stabilize
    agreements without external enforcers.
14. p. 50 — user-monitors outperform hired guards; no statistical
    difference between protected parks and non-parks (Hayes and Ostrom).

== Hooks to the Nostr thesis

- Institutional invisibility (p. 23) is the problem Nostr dissolves:
  rules-in-use become signed typed events — link:claim-formation-observable.adoc[claim-formation-observable].
- Payoff-rule questions "who is responsible for sanctioning... how
  reliably" (p. 39) get a decoupled answer on Nostr — link:claim-split-sanctions.adoc[claim-split-sanctions].
- Rule-ordered producer competition (p. 44) = relay competition —
  link:concept-interjurisdictional-competition.adoc[concept-interjurisdictional-competition].
- Configural rules (pp. 24–25) ground link:claim-multiplicity-stack.adoc[claim-multiplicity-stack].
- Boundary legitimacy + local monitoring over ownership form (pp. 33,
  48, 50) argues for local filtering over global bans — link:claim-no-global-bannability.adoc[claim-no-global-bannability].

== Tensions / open questions

- Design principles distilled from long-surviving institutions (p. 48):
  textual support for link:concept-survivorship-bias.adoc[concept-survivorship-bias].
- Community attributes (p. 43) predict rule formation; Nostr's
  pseudonymous open-entry community strains that causal story.
- Subtractability-centric CPR theory vs Nostr's nonsubtractive content —
  link:q-goods-typology-fit.adoc[q-goods-typology-fit], link:concept-cpr-vs-knowledge-commons.adoc[concept-cpr-vs-knowledge-commons].
- Escape-from-tragedy condition is collective-choice access (p. 32); on
  Nostr exit substitutes for voice — link:concept-exit-voice.adoc[concept-exit-voice].
