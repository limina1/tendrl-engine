== The idea

Property is not one right but a bundle. Schlager & Ostrom (1992) give
five: access, extraction (withdrawal), management, exclusion,
alienation; positions stack them — authorized entrant → authorized
user → claimant → proprietor (all but alienation) → full owner (Hess
& Ostrom 2003, pp. 124–127 — link:src-ideas-artifacts-facilities.adoc[src-ideas-artifacts-facilities]). Key
findings: flow rights and system rights are frequently held by
different actors (126); proprietors sustain resources as well as
owners (126); "no set of property rights work equivalently in all
types of settings" (127). For digital knowledge commons the 2007
chapter extends the bundle to seven, adding *contribution* (right to
add content) and *removal* (right to remove one's artifacts) (UKAC
ch. 3, pp. 52–53 — link:src-analyzing-knowledge-commons.adoc[src-analyzing-knowledge-commons]).

== The Nostr mapping

Per relay: access ` read policy; contribution ` write policy (the
right Hess & Ostrom had to invent for knowledge commons is Nostr's
central lever); extraction ` REQ/fetch; removal ` kind-5 deletion
requests; management/participation = operator config + NIP process;
exclusion ` allowlists, bans, filters; alienation ` selling the
relay. Relay operators are *proprietors*; users on open relays are
authorized users/claimants; pubkeys are effectively non-alienable
(identity can't be sold with its web of trust intact). The
flow-vs-system rights split (126) is exactly authorship decoupled
from hosting.

The schema needs an extra column the sources lack: *enforceable by
whom, at what scope*. Removal is honored per-facility but advisory
at the pool level; exclusion is absolute per relay, impossible
globally — link:claim-no-global-bannability.adoc[claim-no-global-bannability]. Redundant multi-layer
exclusion by uncoordinated proprietors is bundle-holding gone
polycentric — link:concept-polycentricity.adoc[concept-polycentricity], link:claim-multiplicity-stack.adoc[claim-multiplicity-stack] —
and the "no bundle works everywhere" finding (127) predicts the
relay spectrum rather than a single optimal relay policy —
link:claim-relay-spectrum.adoc[claim-relay-spectrum].
