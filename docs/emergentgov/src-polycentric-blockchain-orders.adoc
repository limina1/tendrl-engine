== What it is

Corrective to the static "trustless machine" view: protocols evolve
under INTERNAL forces (constitutional political economy — design,
power concentrations, collective choice) and EXTERNAL forces
(industrial-organization competition among rival networks, plus
states). Evidence: two crisis episodes (block-size/BCH, DAO/ETH-ETC),
a power-law commit chart, a comparative table of rival chains.

== Core claims

1. The received view is "overly static"; protocols "evolve in response
   to users' threat to exit" and polycentric forces (707).
2. Protocols are constitutions: they define network citizenship,
   "determine exit costs and enumerate the participants' powers"
   (708, 711).
3. Protocol change is constitutional and redistributive: block size
   was "a seemingly technical choice [with] significant redistributive
   implications" (712–713).
4. "Protocols cannot amend themselves and adjudicate unanticipated
   conflicts," so "the burden of governance falls on humans and
   usually only a few humans" (713).
5. Developer influence is power-law concentrated (Fig. 1, 714) but
   checked: miners/users must adopt changes; consensus is a
   coordination-game equilibrium; forking is the escape valve — "the
   threat of a new competitor is a strong governing force" (714–715).
6. Competition is external governance: platforms "compete for users
   and, as a result, indirectly govern one another" (715); market
   foot-voting beats electoral majorities (715–716); ordinary "users
   can only influence governance by exiting" (716).
7. Markets for governance may yield more efficient institutions —
   conditional on informed users; under rational ignorance a small
   class of governance specialists is disciplined by exit threat (720).

== As a format model

Corrective-to-static-view template: (1) concede and survey received
institutional praise; (2) mechanism primer for the institutionalist
reader; (3) internal-governance theory via two named crises;
(4) external-governance theory via a comparative table;
(5) polycentric reframing + open research margins. The
internal/external two-force scaffold is the signature, directly
imitable move: Nostr internal = NIP process + per-relay policy;
external = relay/client competition and rival protocols.

== Hooks to the Nostr thesis

- TRANSFERS: internal/external scaffold as organizing frame.
- INVERTS: exit costs — network effects make exit costly (716);
  portable keys/follow lists push Nostr's toward zero; episodic exit
  threat becomes continuous discipline (link:concept-exit-voice.adoc[concept-exit-voice]).
- BREAKS: forking as rare traumatic chain split (712–713); no chain,
  so it degenerates into everyday relay choice/founding —
  link:claim-relay-spectrum.adoc[claim-relay-spectrum], link:claim-expand-contract.adoc[claim-expand-contract].
- BREAKS: no global-consensus requirement — NIPs adopted piecemeal;
  their soft-fork easy case (714–715) generalized protocol-wide.
- TRANSFERS: concentrated maintainer influence checked by
  non-adoption (713–715); governance specialists under rational
  ignorance (720) = relay operators/client devs —
  link:concept-interjurisdictional-competition.adoc[concept-interjurisdictional-competition].
- BREAKS: redistributive token stakes vanish (no token/miners/
  treasury); conflict shifts to attention/moderation/interop; their
  fn17 intrinsic-motivation fallback (714) becomes load-bearing.
- TRANSFERS: claim 4 (713) as quotable bridge to the human governance
  layer of reports/mutes/policies — link:claim-formation-observable.adoc[claim-formation-observable].

== Tensions / open questions

- Polycentricity asserted, not operationalized — no design-principle
  audit (link:concept-polycentricity.adoc[concept-polycentricity], link:concept-design-principles.adoc[concept-design-principles]).
- No user-voice mechanism short of exit (716); kind-1984 fills this
  gap — link:claim-split-sanctions.adoc[claim-split-sanctions].
- Two episodes + a table; no systematic event data. State/legal force
  named but bracketed (715 fn21).
- Exit disciplines only informed users (720); ignorance problem
  raised, not resolved — link:q-does-competition-improve-governance.adoc[q-does-competition-improve-governance].
