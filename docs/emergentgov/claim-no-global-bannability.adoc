:title: No global bannability — the adversarial premise
:type: claim
:status: drafted
:drafted-by: assistant
:tags: boundary, adversarial, premise

Nostr may be the most adversarial social environment that exists: no
global authority, no one can be removed from the network, any relay
can admit anyone. Bad actors are filtered *locally*, never banned
*globally*. The definable "who belongs" boundary behind every commons
Ostrom studied (design principle 1) *structurally cannot exist* here.

And yet the community holds — it coheres, sustains discourse, and
people who have seen the worst of it stay. The research question in
one line: **how is there quality discourse at all?** Every other claim
note is a sub-answer.

Why the premise matters doubly:

- *Analytically*, it is the case the framework never reached — the
  worst-case configuration (see
  link:claim-worst-case-hosts-best-case.adoc[claim-worst-case-hosts-best-case]).
- *Normatively*, non-bannability is what makes the composed bounded
  institutions valuable: a gate-kept community can be write-gated on
  its own relay while its ideas stay freely readable and commentable
  everywhere else — no one is ever excluded from the *conversation*,
  only from a *venue* (see
  link:claim-composable-bounded-institutions.adoc[claim-composable-bounded-institutions]).
