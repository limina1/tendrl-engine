:title: Q: Does interjurisdictional competition yield better governance?
:type: question
:status: open
:drafted-by: assistant
:tags: competition, exit, open-question

The open question deliberately left open (a puzzle, not a checkmark).
Exit-based discipline of relay operators is the mechanism; whether the
competition it creates yields *better governance* depends on the
surrounding institutions (the caution from the corporate-charter
literature — Bratton & McCahery — that competition does not guarantee
better outcomes). Here the surrounding institutions are the rest of
the stack.

Complications from the sources: exit disciplines only when the
governed are informed — rational ignorance produces governance
specialists disciplined by exit threat alone
(link:src-polycentric-blockchain-orders.adoc[src-polycentric-blockchain-orders],
p. 720); voluntary-entry settings substitute perceived legitimacy for
enforcement (link:src-digging-deeper.adoc[src-digging-deeper],
p. 88); and Nostr is the limiting case of near-zero exit cost, where
the discipline operates continuously — does the limit behave
differently in kind?
