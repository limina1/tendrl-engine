== What it is

The GKC framework applied to blockchains: networks that "uniquely
combine features of governments, firms, and commons" (109) ARE
knowledge commons. Category-fit argument (community, dilemmas,
resources, boundaries, property), Bitcoin walked through the GKC
question-battery, comparative research agenda. The closest structural
model for the Nostr paper.

== Core claims

1. Blockchain networks "represent knowledge commons governance,"
   pooling and managing distributed information (108–109).
2. "Commons are distinguished from non-commons by the
   institutionalization of sharing of resources among community
   members" (109). Knowledge commons ≠ open to the public; compatible
   with private ownership (110).
3. "[O]penness and its possible benefits are matters of empirical
   study rather than only premises to be assumed" (113).
4. "It is therefore too simplistic always to refer to 'the
   community' engaged in blockchain governance" (111); network
   attributes are "characteristically 'bottom-up' results of
   collective activity" (112).
5. Provisional dilemma inventory: object identity/integrity,
   spillovers, stagnation, information overload in distributed
   anonymous systems (112).
6. Nested micro-commons: "each layer should be considered both part
   of a macro system... and potentially a micro knowledge commons
   layer in itself" (113).
7. "'[B]oundaries' of property are almost always porous"; boundaries
   do not define the commons (114). NFT value "may derive from the
   character of the community and its governance" (114).
8. "Superior governance": external law is nested, not outside; "the
   divide between external and internal rules is often less
   important than the interaction" (115).
9. Governance "is disciplined by the fact that users can stay and
   participate or can exit"; added exit options "create
   opportunities for voice (A. Berg & Berg, 2020)" (116).
10. Dispute resolution ends in consensus-or-fork (block-size/BCH,
    DAO/ETC); developer/miner power concentration is a concern
    (116–117).

== As a format model

Framework-application template, the classic IJC/GKC genre: (1) claim
the object is a commons; (2) restate the framework as a numbered
question-battery; (3) argue category fit dimension by dimension;
(4) walk ONE case through the framework's steps in order (each §4.x =
one cluster: dilemma → internal rules → legal context →
insider/outsider interactions → dispute resolution); (5) comparative
agenda. A Nostr paper keeping GKC as its spine copies this skeleton,
with the twist that every step is observable as typed events.

== Hooks to the Nostr thesis

- TRANSFERS (foundational): institutionalized sharing of a pooled
  information resource fits Nostr BETTER than Bitcoin — signed events
  replicated across relays, no token wrapper; radicalize their claim
  (link:concept-cpr-vs-knowledge-commons.adoc[concept-cpr-vs-knowledge-commons]).
- TRANSFERS: the pooling acts (publish, replicate, report, mute,
  follow) are themselves signed typed events — the GKC battery gets
  the data pipeline the authors lack; they call for empirical work
  (117), Nostr supplies instrumentation — link:claim-formation-observable.adoc[claim-formation-observable].
- TRANSFERS: porous boundaries (114) → per-relay, overlapping,
  continuously renegotiated membership — link:claim-expand-contract.adoc[claim-expand-contract],
  link:claim-relay-spectrum.adoc[claim-relay-spectrum], link:concept-boundary-rules.adoc[concept-boundary-rules].
- TRANSFERS: nested micro-commons (113) = bounded institutions on the
  open substrate — link:claim-composable-bounded-institutions.adoc[claim-composable-bounded-institutions].
- BREAKS: dilemma inventory anchored in consensus/mining (112, 115);
  Nostr has no consensus — integrity is per-event, the contested pool
  is availability/discoverability (spam, exclusion coordination),
  extending their overload/spillover hypotheses.
- INVERTS: exit still ends in traumatic forks, voice in informal
  debate (116–117); Nostr decomposes it — exit per-relay and cheap,
  voice a typed event, sanction decoupled (link:claim-split-sanctions.adoc[claim-split-sanctions]);
  their Berg & Berg line (116) is the citation bridge —
  link:concept-exit-voice.adoc[concept-exit-voice].
- TRANSFERS: "superior governance" (115) frames state law, app
  stores, DNS/hosting pressure as nested, not external; key-based
  property in identity/event stream coexists with commons governance
  (114 analogue).

== Tensions / open questions

- §4's "empirical study" is a literature synthesis; the promised
  program (117) is unexecuted — the opening for a Nostr paper.
- Dilemma inventory admittedly hypothetical, never tested (112).
- Unresolved whether token property strengthens or dilutes commons
  character (§3.6) — link:q-goods-typology-fit.adoc[q-goods-typology-fit].
- Heterogeneity flagged (111) yet Bitcoin narrated as dev/miner/user
  blocs — link:q-boundary-sybil.adoc[q-boundary-sybil].
- Sits in Allen et al.'s "romantic" camp while sharing an author with
  Source 1 — link:concept-tier2-triangle.adoc[concept-tier2-triangle].
