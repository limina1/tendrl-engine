== What it is

An IAD-based analysis of online creation communities (OCCs) — 50-case
statistical sample plus four case studies (Wikipedia, Wikihow, Flickr,
Openesf) — arguing that infrastructure provision, ignored by prior
governance literature, is the dominant and non-neutral governance
dimension of digital commons.

== Core claims

1. p. 1 — OCCs: "communities of individuals that mainly interact via a
   platform of online participation, with the goal of building and
   sharing a common-pool resource."
2. p. 11 — eight governance dimensions: mission; social norms; platform
   design ("where regulation is embedded in the code"); self-management
   of contributions; formal rules; license; decision-making/conflict
   resolution; infrastructure provision.
3. pp. 30–31 — infrastructure provision determines several of the other
   dimensions (design, license, formal policies, sometimes mission and
   conflict decisions); only self-management escapes provider control.
4. pp. 27–30 — two axes: open vs closed community involvement in the
   provider body; net-enabler (copyleft+FLOSS, forkable) vs black box
   (proprietary). Four models: corporate (Flickr), mission enterprise
   (Wikihow), representational foundation (Wikipedia), assembly (Openesf).
5. pp. 25–26 — "Forkability empowers the community with respect to the
   infrastructure provider"; 68.1% free content licenses, 78% free
   software in the 50-case sample.
6. pp. 18–23 — participation self-allocated, 90/9/1 power law; OCCs are
   anti-rival — "the value of the outcome of OCCs increases when more
   people use them"; free riding is not a dilemma.
7. pp. 15, 17 — openness and transparency embedded in code (80% open
   registration; 88% publicly readable communications); public process
   enables coordination "without a predefined plan or a gatekeeper."
8. pp. 33–35 — tradeoff: black-box dependency grows size but hurts
   collaboration; net-enabler autonomy favors complex collaboration but
   smaller size; "none of the models combine a large community size
   with collaborativeness."
9. pp. 35–36 — vs Ostrom's design principles: "higher self-governance
   favors collaboration, but not a high level of participation."
10. pp. 37–38 — IAD amendment: contra Schweik & English, "rules embedded
    in the code govern the interaction as much as other rules" — code is
    governance, not a resource attribute.
11. pp. 39–41 — stricter digital-commons definition contra Benkler: open
    access alone is insufficient; add "a governance design that
    maintains community control over the collaborative process."
    Flickr fails the test; for-profit Wikihow can pass.

== Hooks to the Nostr thesis

- Nostr disaggregates dimension (viii) itself: plural substitutable
  relays mean no single provider controls design/rules/conflict for the
  commons — link:concept-infrastructure-provision.adoc[concept-infrastructure-provision], link:claim-no-global-bannability.adoc[claim-no-global-bannability].
- Her four provision models become live market structure inside one
  system — link:claim-relay-spectrum.adoc[claim-relay-spectrum], link:claim-multiplicity-stack.adoc[claim-multiplicity-stack].
- Forkability-as-power generalizes: identity+graph portability makes
  forking continuous and per-user — link:concept-interjurisdictional-competition.adoc[concept-interjurisdictional-competition].
- Size-vs-collaborativeness tradeoff = testable prediction across the
  relay spectrum; bounded relays on an open substrate may evade it —
  link:claim-composable-bounded-institutions.adoc[claim-composable-bounded-institutions], link:q-does-competition-improve-governance.adoc[q-does-competition-improve-governance].
- Her commons test (community control over means of production) gives a
  criterion for arguing Nostr is a commons proper —
  link:q-goods-typology-fit.adoc[q-goods-typology-fit].

== Tensions / open questions

- Assumes one provider per community; Nostr's many-providers-per-user
  breaks her unit of analysis — link:claim-expand-contract.adoc[claim-expand-contract].
- Action situations explicitly not analyzed (pp. 33, 36): static
  comparative snapshot, the anti-model of link:claim-formation-observable.adoc[claim-formation-observable] —
  link:concept-action-arena.adoc[concept-action-arena].
- No machinery for boundaries/exclusion dynamics (p. 8) — link:concept-boundary-rules.adoc[concept-boundary-rules], link:q-boundary-sybil.adoc[q-boundary-sybil].
- "The community" is ill-defined on Nostr (overlapping publics): whose
  control counts? — link:q-decoupled-vs-unitary-sanctions.adoc[q-decoupled-vs-unitary-sanctions].
