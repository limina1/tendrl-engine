:title: Split sanctions — signal and enforcement decoupled
:type: claim
:status: drafted
:drafted-by: assistant
:tags: sanctions, kind-1984, decoupling, novelty

The cleanest novelty for institutional economics. Ostrom's graduated
sanctions (design principle 5) were *unitary*: whoever sanctions also
enforces. Nostr splits them. A report (kind 1984) is a costly, typed,
public, **non-binding** signal — and a *different* actor decides what
it means: a relay filters on it, a client scores it into a web of
trust, a user ignores it.

In ADICO terms the report is an institutional statement with no "Or
Else" of its own; the O is crowdsourced by uncoordinated enforcers
(link:concept-adico-grammar.adoc[concept-adico-grammar]). In
cybernetic terms the signal is the information flow and someone else
closes the loop
(link:concept-adaptive-governance.adoc[concept-adaptive-governance]).
Whether decoupling governs better or worse than unitary enforcement is
the open, publishable question:
link:q-decoupled-vs-unitary-sanctions.adoc[q-decoupled-vs-unitary-sanctions].

Kept-ready concrete observation: high-centrality reporters appear to
trigger relay filtering while peripheral ones do not — makes the
thread concrete without overselling the data.
