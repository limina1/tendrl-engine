:title: Q: Does decoupled sanctioning govern better or worse than unitary?
:type: question
:status: open
:date: 2026-08-04
:tags: ostrom-extension, sanctions

Ostrom extension #2 — the most self-contained publishable question,
native to institutional economics, co-designable. Graduated sanctions
in the CPR literature are unitary (monitor and sanctioner
institutionally linked); Nostr severs signal (kind-1984 report,
public, costly, non-binding) from enforcement (relay, client, user —
each optional, uncoordinated).

Sub-questions: Under what conditions does a non-binding signal get
enforced at all (enforcer incentives)? Is emergent aggregate
gradualism (many small independent enforcements) functionally
equivalent to designed graduated sanctions? Does decoupling avoid the
crowding-out of self-imposed sanctions that external enforcement
causes (link:src-ostrom-irc-iad.adoc[src-ostrom-irc-iad], p. 48)?
Concrete anomaly in hand: high-centrality reporters appear to trigger
relay filtering; peripheral reporters do not — legitimacy of the
monitor as the binding variable
(link:src-analyzing-knowledge-commons.adoc[src-analyzing-knowledge-commons],
p. 67).
