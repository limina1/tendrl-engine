== What it is

FILE-IDENTITY CORRECTION: despite the filename and the project email,
this PDF is NOT Frischmann/Madison/Strandburg, Governing Knowledge
Commons (OUP 2014). It is the 2023 Cambridge smart-cities volume in the
same series. Its ch. 1 is the mature statement of the GKC research
framework (Figure 1.1 reproduces Madison/Frischmann/Strandburg 2010;
Table 1.1 adapts Sanfilippo/Frischmann/Strandburg 2018), so it serves
as the framework source. ACQUISITION TARGETS for citation: the 2014 OUP
volume (its framework ch. 1) and MFS 2010, "Constructing Commons in the
Cultural Environment," Cornell L. Rev. 95:657. Only ch. 1 + front
matter read; other chapters unread.

== Core claims (ch. 1 only; printed pages)

1. p. 7 — thesis: smart-city knowledge/data "require governance,
   especially governance of data-focused intelligence and
   intelligence-enabled control."
2. p. 13 — "intelligence lies in and through the data, rather than in
   and through the people"; data representing people demand governance.
3. p. 15 — knowledge commons = institutions for community governance of
   shared knowledge resources; commons is a governance mode, not a good
   type; "institutionalized sharing" is the distinguishing mark.
4. pp. 15–16 — vs Ostrom's CPR premise: relevant social dilemmas are "a
   question for research in a specific context, rather than a premise to
   be assumed"; commons governance can itself create new obstacles.
5. p. 17 (Fig. 1.1) — action arenas as core unit; feedback loop: outputs
   are community-defined resources that re-enter as inputs, justifying
   community-level (not user-level) analysis.
6. p. 17 — arena calibration: too broad = no fixed exogenous elements;
   too narrow = no meaningful patterns.
7. p. 18 — evaluation against internally defined goals; no external
   normative yardstick; legitimacy and appropriateness of information
   flows as candidate criteria.
8. pp. 20–21 — key themes: recursive community constitution; dilemmas
   identified first; non-knowledge resources matter; assessment turns on
   participation pragmatics incl. "possibilities for exit."
9. Table 1.1 (pp. 19–20) — four question-clusters: background
   environment _ attributes _ governance / patterns & outcomes.
