== The idea

Boundary rules "specify how participants enter or leave... positions"
(McGinnis p. 10); Ostrom's chapter calls them entry and exit rules:
they set "the number of participants, their attributes and resources,
whether they can enter freely, and the conditions they face for
leaving" (2007, p. 38). Her CPR battery lists the observed variety:
residency, ascriptive group, lottery, permit, asset ownership, entry
fee or initiation, forfeit-on-migration (pp. 38–39). Clear boundaries
are design principle 1 (McGinnis p. 19), and empirically it is
_agreement about the legitimacy of boundaries_, plus reliable
monitoring — not ownership form — that predicts cooperation (Ostrom
p. 33). The capacity to enter or exit a group is itself a cooperation
booster in the experimental literature (McGinnis p. 22).

== The Nostr engagement

The relay spectrum is a boundary-rule dial: open pool (default
conditions — no boundary rule), paid relays (entry fee), WoT relays
(attribute test via follows), allowlist/gated relays (permit),
single-team relays (ascriptive) — link:claim-relay-spectrum.adoc[claim-relay-spectrum]. Because each
relay sets boundary rules only for itself, boundaries exist everywhere
locally and nowhere globally: link:claim-no-global-bannability.adoc[claim-no-global-bannability]. The
"boundary as a muscle" motif — expand under growth, contract under
spam/attack, re-expand — is repeated re-parameterization of one rule
type, cheap because relay-level, and fully logged
(link:claim-expand-contract.adoc[claim-expand-contract], link:claim-formation-observable.adoc[claim-formation-observable]). Strains:
pseudonymous keys make attribute-based boundaries sybil-vulnerable
(link:q-boundary-sybil.adoc[q-boundary-sybil]), and exit rules are vacuous — no one can be made
to forfeit an identity. Wider field inventory of boundary-rule variety:
link:src-digging-deeper.adoc[src-digging-deeper].
