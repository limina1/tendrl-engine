== The idea

Self-appointed guardians whose authority rests on observable behavior
and reputation, not on any office. Schroeder & Wagner's strongest
finding: "individuals acting as guardian of article" was rated
important by 83% — the highest of all 24 mechanisms (Table 1,
p. 1954) — despite guardians holding no formal role. Their authority
is legitimized the way Tyler describes: peers accept influence from
those whose visible conduct earns it (p. 1955). This grounds their
anti-equipotentiality conclusion: "equipotentiality oversimplifies
the complex mechanisms that control and guide the creation of open
content" (1955) — flat access does not make flat power.

On Nostr the pattern recurs under a maximally flat protocol: no
protocol role distinguishes any keypair, yet power gradients are
steep — accounts with large follow counts steer norms, prolific
client devs decide which mechanisms exist by shipping them, and
big-relay operators guard de facto public squares. The Nostr
advantage for study: the observable behavior that legitimizes a
guardian is cryptographically observable — signed, typed, and
complete — so guardianship can be measured rather than recalled.

Strain: Wikipedia's guardians guard a shared singular artifact (the
article); Nostr guardians guard views — a mute list or relay policy
binds only its subscribers. Guardianship without office becomes
guardianship without a unique object, redundant and uncoordinated
across actors.
