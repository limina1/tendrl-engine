== The idea

Many formally independent decision centres with overlapping
jurisdictions, taking each other into account, under an overarching
system of rules. Its payoff is built-in self-correction: polycentric
systems are "more likely than monocentric systems to provide
incentives leading to self-organized, self-corrective institutional
change" (Ostrom 1998, in Lewis & Aligica 2024, p. 3). Competition
among overlapping providers generates error-correcting negative
feedback as an unintended consequence (p. 4); Lewis & Aligica recast
the whole architecture cybernetically as "countervailing controls
based on multi-level feedback systems" whose capacity for
self-governance is an emergent property (pp. 10–11). Overlap also
carries information: what worked in one unit transmits to others, and
larger systems back up failing small ones (fn. 14, p. 11).

Citation discipline: cite Lewis & Aligica 2024 (pp. 3–4, 10–11),
McGinnis's guide (pp. 6–7), or Vincent Ostrom directly. Do NOT cite
Digging Deeper — that chapter never treats polycentricity by name;
its nearest passage is one paragraph on producer competition (Ostrom
2005/2025, p. 96).

== The Nostr strain

Nostr is polycentric in structure — thousands of relay operators,
client authors, list curators, no centre — but classical
polycentricity assumes the centres *interact*: negotiation,
contracting, mutual adjustment among authorities. Relay operators
mostly never coordinate; the protocol itself is the coordination
medium, replacing negotiated interaction with typed-event
interoperability (link:concept-governance-as-software.adoc[concept-governance-as-software]). And the
redundancy is uncoordinated: many actors independently solve the same
exclusion problem in parallel layers, which is polycentric
self-correction without the inter-centre bargaining the theory
presumes (link:claim-multiplicity-stack.adoc[claim-multiplicity-stack]). Whether protocol-mediated
mutual adjustment still yields the self-corrective dividend is an
open question — link:q-does-competition-improve-governance.adoc[q-does-competition-improve-governance].
