== The idea

Fuster Morell's central finding: of the eight governance dimensions of
online creation communities, infrastructure provision is the one that
determines several of the others — platform design, license, formal
policies, sometimes mission and conflict decisions (pp. 30–31) — and it
is not neutral (pp. 40–41). Provision varies on two axes: community
involvement in the provider body (open↔closed) and community autonomy
from the platform (net-enabler: copyleft+FLOSS, forkable ↔ black box:
proprietary), yielding four models — corporate (Flickr), mission
enterprise (Wikihow), representational foundation (Wikipedia), assembly
self-provision (Openesf) (pp. 27–30). Forkability is the master power
variable: a community that can replicate the platform and leave is free
from its provider (pp. 25–26). Empirical tradeoff: black-box dependency
grows community size but depresses collaboration; net-enabler autonomy
enables complex collaboration but smaller communities; no model gets
both (pp. 33–35).

== The Nostr case

Nostr disaggregates the dimension itself: infrastructure provision is
plural and substitutable (many relays per user, identity and social
graph portable), so no provider controls design, rules, or conflict
resolution for the commons as a whole. Fuster Morell's cross-case
typology becomes live market structure inside one system — corporate,
enterprise, foundation, and assembly-style relays coexist on the same
substrate (link:claim-relay-spectrum.adoc[claim-relay-spectrum]), and "forking" is continuous,
per-user relay exit rather than a rare community schism. Her tradeoff
result becomes a testable prediction: open-pool relays should show
Flickr-like scale with thin collaboration, gate-kept bounded relays
(e.g. a write-gated medical knowledge relay) foundation/assembly-like
intensive collaboration — and composition on one substrate may evade
her "no model combines both."
