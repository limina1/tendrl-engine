== What it is

Elinor Ostrom's canonical statement of the IAD method: universal
components of all markets and hierarchies; the action situation as
dependent variable; seven rule types; nested levels of analysis.
VERIFICATION: the 2025 Springer chapter is the 2005 first-edition
classic reprinted unchanged (front-page note confirms the posthumous
reprint) — cite as Ostrom 2005/2025. FLAGS: this chapter does NOT use
"holons" (that is Understanding Institutional Diversity ch. 1) and
does NOT treat polycentricity by name (nearest: producer competition,
one paragraph, p. 96) — do not cite it for either.

== Core claims

1. p. 78 — "Institutions themselves are usually invisible": shared
   concepts "in the minds of participants," often implicit, unwritten.
2. pp. 79, 86 — rules _ norms _ strategies distinguished; rules are
   enforced "in a predictable way by agents responsible for
   monitoring conduct and for imposing sanctions"; rules-in-use "may
   actually be contrary to" the rules-in-form of written documents.
3. p. 80 — rule effects are configural, non-additive: one rule's
   impact "is not independent of the configuration of other rules."
4. pp. 82–84, 88–89 — action arena = situation (seven variable
   clusters) + actors; digging deeper, the situation becomes the
   dependent variable, structured by rules-in-use, biophysical world,
   and community, with seven rule types matched one-to-one to the
   seven situation components.
5. pp. 90–91 — THE CENTERPIECE: "At least 27 different boundary rules
   are in use in different locations in the management of common-pool
   resources"; and "The set of identified rules is large enough that
   no analyst can perform a complete analysis of the structure of
   resulting situations"; next frontier = how rules combine.
6. p. 90 — boundary-rule changes determine whether a market is
   "competitive, oligopolistic, or monopolistic."
7. p. 87 — "rules are not self-formulating, self-determining, or
   self-enforcing" (Ostrom 1980); unpunished cheating cascades.
8. p. 88 — where participants freely enter and leave, rules persist
   only via a shared sense that they are appropriate — legitimacy
   substitutes for enforcement in voluntary settings.
9. p. 86 — "it is always possible to rescind it or reinstate it—or
   it is not a rule."
10. p. 96 — "All rules are nested in another set of rules... similar
    to the nesting of computer languages"; deeper-level change is
    harder and costlier, stabilizing expectations; four levels
    (operational → meta-constitutional), Fig. 5.3 p. 97.
11. pp. 92–95 — excludability × subtractability goods typology;
    community attributes (shared norms, homogeneity, multiplex ties)
    ease rule-devising.
12. p. 99 — changed rule configurations create "new and unanalyzed
    situations" demanding fresh analysis of how participants view,
    monitor, and enforce the new rules.

== Hooks to the Nostr thesis

- The 27+ boundary rules / "no analyst" passage (pp. 90–91) is the
  direct ancestor of "unenumerability is the finding": Nostr's
  exclusion-method space is the same open-endedness, machine-readable
  and self-renewing — link:claim-multiplicity-stack.adoc[claim-multiplicity-stack], link:concept-requisite-variety.adoc[concept-requisite-variety].
- Invisibility inverted (p. 78): on Nostr rules-in-use have a wire
  format — link:concept-institutional-invisibility.adoc[concept-institutional-invisibility], link:claim-formation-observable.adoc[claim-formation-observable].
- Boundary rules set market structure (p. 90) ↔ relay spectrum —
  link:claim-relay-spectrum.adoc[claim-relay-spectrum], link:claim-expand-contract.adoc[claim-expand-contract].
- Voluntary-entry legitimacy (p. 88) grounds exit-discipline and the
  viability of non-binding reports — link:concept-exit-voice.adoc[concept-exit-voice], link:claim-split-sanctions.adoc[claim-split-sanctions].
- Computer-language nesting (p. 96) is nearly literal: NIPs → relay
  policy → moderation events — link:concept-governance-as-software.adoc[concept-governance-as-software].
- p. 99 prescribes exactly the study the project performs — but Ostrom
  could only do it retrospectively — link:concept-survivorship-bias.adoc[concept-survivorship-bias].

- Community-attribute prerequisites (p. 95) look unfavorable for
  pseudonymous Nostr, yet governance emerges — link:q-boundary-sybil.adoc[q-boundary-sybil], link:q-maturation-hypothesis.adoc[q-maturation-hypothesis].
