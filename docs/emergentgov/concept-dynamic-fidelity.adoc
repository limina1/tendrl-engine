== The idea

Alston's coinage: when authority to change a protocol is mechanically
distributed among independent actor classes (validators, developers,
users, complementors), protocol-level commitments become credibly
entrenched — emergent checks and balances analogous to constitutional
separation of powers (pp. 1, 12–14). The mechanism is North & Weingast's
Glorious Revolution logic: credibility rests on architectural
separation, not good intentions; centralized-platform users remain
"pre-Glorious Revolution lenders" (p. 13). Auditability reinforces it:
publicly legible rules plus an auditable application history let
participants detect deviations, while entrenchment prevents deviations
being imposed — jointly collapsing Ostrom's monitoring costs
(pp. 17–18). Contrast class: procedural constraint (platform policies,
appeals) inside centralized discretion, suited to context-specific rule
application; architectural constraint suits credible commitment
(pp. 5–6). For expressive/associative protocols, only a narrow core
needs entrenchment — "permissionless access, pseudonymous
participation, and non-discretionary execution" — with flexibility
elsewhere (p. 20). Costs: entrenchment protects unjust rules equally,
and de facto power can relocate to adjacent microstructure layers even
when core rules stay distributed (pp. 15–17).

== The Nostr case

Nostr fits the expressive-protocol specification almost exactly: the
entrenched core is the identity/portability layer (keys, signatures,
event formats — hence no global bannability), while the relay-policy
layer is maximally flexible and discretionary (procedural constraint,
relay by relay). But Nostr lacks Alston's entrenchment engine — no
consensus mechanism, no staked validators. What entrenches the core is
implementation plurality: many independent clients and relays plus the
NIP process, i.e. his complementor-veto logic without validators.
Whether that is durable is open (link:q-generativity-robustness.adoc[q-generativity-robustness]); and his
microstructure caveat transfers as concentration in big relays, default
clients, and discovery services.
