This kasten maps the sources and ideas of the *emergent governance on
Nostr* research program: institutional analysis (Ostrom, IAD, knowledge
commons) applied to a commons that can be *watched governing itself
into existence* — every report, mute, follow, and relay-policy change a
signed, timestamped, typed event — instead of reverse-engineering rules
from institutions that already succeeded. Ostrom's design principles
were survivorship-filtered; formation was never observable. On Nostr it
is. That inversion is the center of gravity; everything here connects
to it.

The corpus is centered on link:map-email.adoc[Jayme's source email]
(`sources/email.md`) — background theory, format-model papers, and
related work — plus the project's own articulation of the thesis
(`sources/presentation.tex`, `sources/orientation.md`) and selected
chapters of _Understanding Knowledge as a Commons_ (UKAC).

Standing distinctions this kasten must not blur:

- *Two papers, kept separate.* The commons-governance paper (Ostrom is
  the _message_) is this kasten's subject. The public-health / medical
  knowledge commons paper (Ostrom is the _method_) enters only as the
  bounded institution that composes onto the open substrate once the
  substrate is proven — globally readable and commentable, write-gated
  on its own relay, excludable nowhere.
- *Observation in real time, not autopsy.* The empirical claim is
  about watching formation, not classifying outcomes ("look what is
  happening!" is the failure mode the founders themselves named).
- *The unenumerable space.* Filtering/exclusion methods are samples
  from an open, self-renewing space; cataloguing "the N mechanisms"
  reintroduces the taxonomy trap.

What this kasten is not: a summary of whole books (sources stay
external; notes carry citations, not text), a draft of either paper,
or a pitch script (that lives in `sources/orientation.md`).
