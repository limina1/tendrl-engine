== The idea

IAD sits at the most general of three levels of specificity. A
_framework_ identifies the universal elements any relevant theory must
address and supplies a metatheoretical language for comparing theories;
a _theory_ makes working assumptions about which elements matter; a
_model_ fixes precise parameters (Ostrom 2007, pp. 25–26; McGinnis
p. 4). Many theories fit one framework, many models fit one theory.

The diagram logic (Ostrom 2007, Fig. 2.1 p. 27; McGinnis pp. 7–8,
Fig. 1 p. 27): three clusters of exogenous variables — (1) rules-in-use,
(2) biophysical/material conditions ("attributes of states of the
world"), (3) attributes of the community — jointly structure an action
arena, which generates patterns of interaction and outcomes, which are
assessed by evaluative criteria and feed back on every component. The
three clusters combine configurally, not additively (Ostrom pp. 24–25):
working rules alone are never a sufficient explanation (p. 37).
McGinnis adds that the whole is a system of nested, "nearly
decomposable" action situations (p. 8).

== The Nostr engagement

The thesis uses IAD as the diagnostic grid but inverts its epistemics:
IAD was built because institutions are invisible and must be elicited;
on Nostr the exogenous-variable cluster "rules-in-use" is materialized
as signed events and machine-readable relay policy, so the framework's
categories can be populated by observation rather than interview
(link:claim-formation-observable.adoc[claim-formation-observable], link:claim-interviews-carry-claim.adoc[claim-interviews-carry-claim]).
Biophysical conditions map to protocol/code constraints
(link:concept-governance-as-software.adoc[concept-governance-as-software]); community attributes are the
strained cluster — pseudonymous, open-entry, no shared culture.
