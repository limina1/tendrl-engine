== The idea

Schneider's critique of the default political architecture of online
community software: platforms assign "unchecked power to admins and
moderators" (Modular Politics 16:1, via ref [121], Schneider's
"Admins, Mods, and Benevolent Dictators for Life"). Because
alternatives are costly and must be built outside the platform,
"monarchic or oligarchic practices emerge in online networks more
easily and frequently than liberal or democratic ones" (16:2). The
feudalism is implicit: nobody legislates autocracy, the feature-set
does — juries, parties, term limits simply are not shipped.

The Nostr twist: the feudal root is abolished globally — no admin
anywhere can ban a keypair from the network — yet feudalism reappears
fractally at the relay level, where each operator is absolute lord of
their allowlist. Micro-feudal domains, disciplined by frictionless
exit rather than by voice, compose into a whole that is not feudal:
no lord of lords. This sharpens Schneider's own concession that
pliable governance tools "can allow the implementation of oppressive
regimes" (16:2) — on Nostr the oppression is bounded by the domain,
and the domain is bounded by exit.

Open question: is fractal feudalism a stable equilibrium or a
transitional phase — do relay-level lords accrete network-level power
through scale (Damus, Primal) despite the flat protocol?
