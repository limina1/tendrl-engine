== What it is

Repertory-grid study (12 English-Wikipedia editors, 154 raw → 65
consolidated → 24 shared constructs) enumerating the control and
guiding mechanisms of everyday open content creation, arguing that
informal mechanisms dominate and formal ones are a rarely-invoked
backstop.

== Core claims

1. p. 1948 — formal mechanisms "are only called upon in very few
   instances"; light informal governance coexists with formal structure.
2. p. 1948 — governance widened beyond deviance-prevention to the
   structures, processes, and roles guiding routine content creation.
3. p. 1949 — social control theory: formalizing norms risks
   "independent artifacts that are decoupled from the dynamic processes
   of social norm formation."
4. pp. 1952–1954 — 24 shared mechanisms; 8 rated important (≥50% of
   ratings ≥4, Table 1 at 1954): guardian of the article (83%, highest),
   facilitation by experienced users (67%), policies (56%), talk-page
   discussions (55%), large number of editors, collaboration among
   users, inviting individuals to participate, participation by highly
   reputable user (each 50%). Remaining 16, one line: administrator
   involvement; arbitration; featured-article process; formal review;
   formal mediation; formal voting; guidelines; reverting; consensus
   building; coordination; user-talk discussions; feedback; informal
   mediation; informal networks; mentoring; personal relationships.
5. p. 1955 — of the formal class "only policies were assessed as
   important"; informal mechanisms are the responsive, fast-evolving
   everyday layer.
6. p. 1955 — "formal governance mechanisms are triggered once informal
   mechanisms have been exhausted" — escalation backstop; rarely-invoked
   rules still communicate norms.
7. p. 1955 — anti-equipotentiality: "equipotentiality oversimplifies
   the complex mechanisms"; guardians and reputable users wield outsized
   influence legitimized by observable behavior (Tyler), not office.
8. pp. 1955–1956 — environments of governance: mechanisms unfold in
   three platform-designed spaces (article, talk, user pages) whose
   design determines which mechanisms can emerge; off-platform channels
   "limit involvement and reduce transparency."
9. p. 1957 — limitation (f): "data collected are retrospective and
   therefore carry the inherent risk of memory bias."

== Hooks to the Nostr thesis

- The retrospective-memory-bias limitation (1957) is removed on Nostr:
  every mute/report/policy change is a typed signed event —
  link:claim-formation-observable.adoc[claim-formation-observable].
- Their catalogue is a closed enumeration on one platform; Nostr's
  mechanism space is unenumerable — any dev mints new mechanisms as
  software — link:q-generativity-robustness.adoc[q-generativity-robustness].
- Repertory grids are replicable on Nostr, with event traces replacing
  recall — pairs with link:claim-interviews-carry-claim.adoc[claim-interviews-carry-claim].
- Formal-as-backstop ≈ kind-1984: non-binding signals, enforcement
  elsewhere — link:claim-split-sanctions.adoc[claim-split-sanctions], link:concept-graduated-sanctions.adoc[concept-graduated-sanctions].
- Environments of governance: Wikipedia's three are platform-fixed;
  Nostr's are permissionlessly extensible event kinds —
  link:claim-composable-bounded-institutions.adoc[claim-composable-bounded-institutions].

== Tensions / open questions

- One platform, one language, n=12; taxonomy may be Wikipedia-shaped.
- Exit is absent from the catalogue entirely — the mechanism class
  central to Nostr — link:concept-exit-voice.adoc[concept-exit-voice].
- Shared/idiosyncratic filter (1952–1953) may not transfer: on Nostr
  one relay's idiosyncratic policy still binds locally —
  link:claim-relay-spectrum.adoc[claim-relay-spectrum].
- Formal/informal binary strains when norms ship as code —
  link:concept-governance-as-software.adoc[concept-governance-as-software].
- Mechanisms are perceptions, not behaviors; importance conflates
  salience with efficacy.
