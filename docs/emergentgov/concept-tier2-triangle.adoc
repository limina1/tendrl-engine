== The idea

The three tier-2 papers frame the same object three ways: blockchain
governance as knowledge commons (Murtazashvili/Murtazashvili/Weiss/
Madison — GKC question-battery, institutionalized sharing), as
public-choice exchange (Allen/Berg/Lane/MacDonald/Potts — bargaining
under uncertainty, governance innovation as value creation), and as
constitutional-plus-competitive polycentric order (Alston/Law/
Murtazashvili/Weiss — internal constitutional politics disciplined by
external market competition). This is a live IN-FAMILY debate, not
three silos: Ilia Murtazashvili co-authors the commons and the
constitutional papers; Allen et al. explicitly file the
Ostrom/IAD/GKC line — citing the Bodon et al. companion of Source 3's
authors — in the "romantic" camp (661), while all three share
polycentricity vocabulary and load-bearing cites (Berg & Berg 2020;
Alston 2020; De Filippi & Loveluck 2016; Ostrom; Aligica & Tarko).
Writing into this triangle means entering an ongoing conversation,
not choosing a school.

== The Nostr synthesis

The Nostr paper can take one leg from each: GKC as descriptive spine
(Source 3's framework-walk skeleton), competition-and-exit as the
disciplining mechanism (Source 1's internal/external scaffold), and
permissionless entrepreneurial layering as the dynamic (Source 2's
Lido/Metagov logic) — answering the "romantic" jab with the
descriptive-instrument move: commons frameworks measure observed
emergent order, they don't prescribe designed consensus
(link:src-exchange-theory-web3.adoc[src-exchange-theory-web3]). Two devices fall out. First, a
mechanism-mapping table: for each mechanism in the three papers
(fork, token vote, miner consensus, exit threat, nested commons…),
mark whether it TRANSFERS, INVERTS, or VANISHES on Nostr — every
vanishing entry is price/stake coordination replaced by
reputation/attention/replication. Second, the evidence contrast: all
three papers lean on the field's same two war stories (block-size/BCH
and DAO/ETC — retrospective, rare, chain-level), whereas Nostr yields
continuous, typed, signed governance events
(link:claim-formation-observable.adoc[claim-formation-observable]). The triangle's shared blind spot —
no systematic event data, promised empirics unexecuted — is exactly
the opening.
