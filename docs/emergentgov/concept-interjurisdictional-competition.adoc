== The idea

Alston et al.'s external-force half: governance providers are
themselves governed by the market they compete in. "Software platforms
compete for users and, as a result, indirectly govern one another"
(715); rival networks differentiate on quality AND on governance
features themselves — consensus mechanisms as "competing sets of
governance choices" (717–720). The market beats the electoral channel
because each user consumes the product of their own choice rather than
the majority's (715–716). The claimed payoff is a market for
governance: institutional competition for voluntary support "may yield
more efficient institutions" (720). But the claim is CONDITIONAL:
it needs informed governed parties and supportive surrounding
institutions; under rational political ignorance, most users don't
monitor rules, leaving a small class of governance specialists
(core devs, miners) disciplined mainly by the aggregate exit threat
(720). Murtazashvili et al. echo the mechanism — altcoin entry and
user exit discipline Bitcoin (116) — and Allen et al. supply its
dynamic version: permissionless entry into governance provision as
entrepreneurial discovery (663–664).

== The Nostr engagement

Relay competition is this concept at high frequency and low stakes:
relay operators and client devs are Nostr's governance specialists,
users are rationally ignorant of relay policy, and the discipline is
exit — but with near-zero switching costs and, crucially, a
REGENERATING set of jurisdictions: exit routinely founds new relays
rather than only reallocating share among incumbents
(link:concept-exit-voice.adoc[concept-exit-voice], link:claim-relay-spectrum.adoc[claim-relay-spectrum]). Strains: (a) the
informed-user condition is even less satisfied on Nostr, yet every
policy change and defection is a logged typed event, so whether
competition actually improves governance becomes empirically testable
rather than assumed (link:claim-formation-observable.adoc[claim-formation-observable],
link:q-does-competition-improve-governance.adoc[q-does-competition-improve-governance]); (b) with no token revenue,
what operators compete FOR (attention, reputation, subscription fees,
ideology) is itself an open variable; (c) multihoming — users on many
relays at once — blurs the "jurisdiction" unit that the
interjurisdictional frame presumes (link:claim-multiplicity-stack.adoc[claim-multiplicity-stack]).
