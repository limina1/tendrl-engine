== The idea

The action arena is "the social space where individuals interact,
exchange goods and services, solve problems, dominate one another, or
fight" (Ostrom 2007, p. 28). It contains an _action situation_ plus the
_actors_ (each actor: resources, valuations, information-processing,
selection processes; p. 28). The action situation has seven working
components: participants, positions, allowable actions and their
outcome linkages, potential outcomes, control over choice, information,
and costs/benefits (Ostrom pp. 28–29; McGinnis p. 9, framed as "a
generalization of the 'rules of a game'"). Ostrom 2010 drops the
arena/situation distinction, folding actors in via position rules
(McGinnis p. 7).

McGinnis preserves the crucial _default conditions_ (p. 10, from OGW):
absent specified rules there is one position, any physically possible
action, independent action, physical aggregation, full information, and
"any player can retain any outcome that the player can physically
obtain and defend."

== The Nostr engagement

The open relay pool approximates the default conditions: any keypair
(one position: "user"), any physically possible action (any signed
event propagates), retention by physical possession (your key, your
events). Every governance structure observed on Nostr is a departure
from these defaults — gated relays add positions (operator, member),
information rules, and boundary rules on top of the substrate without
removing it (link:claim-composable-bounded-institutions.adoc[claim-composable-bounded-institutions],
link:claim-worst-case-hosts-best-case.adoc[claim-worst-case-hosts-best-case]). Each relay is its own action
situation; the stack of relays + clients + label providers is the
linked, nearly decomposable system McGinnis describes (p. 8) —
link:claim-multiplicity-stack.adoc[claim-multiplicity-stack].
