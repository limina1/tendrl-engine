:title: Interviews carry the claim; the trace locates
:type: claim
:status: drafted
:date: 2026-08-04
:tags: method, interviews, traces, inversion

A deliberate methodological inversion from an earlier draft — respect
it. The event trace can *locate* an adaptation in time, sometimes
catch its speed: a bonus, not load-bearing. Much of the redundancy
(relay software, infrastructure, client web-of-trust, interop specs)
never appears in public events at all.

The interviews are primary because what is being established is a
*community disposition*: people orient to an open problem with no
global solution and keep adapting anyway — observable only through
lived testimony plus realized outputs. The signal question: why are
people who have seen the worst of Nostr still here? Why not retreat
permanently to the safe one-relay community? **That they stay is the
evidence the adaptive space is winning.** Refusing the global solution
is the finding, not a gap.

Standing discipline #2: "everyone adapts" never stands alone without a
NAMED INSTANCE — a spec that emerged after a specific wave, a
hackathon entry against a named threat, an operator who tightened then
loosened. The instance keeps it falsifiable.

Supporting methods precedent: Schroeder & Wagner's repertory-grid
elicitation is replicable on Nostr, minus their retrospective
memory-bias limitation

the civic-sphere caveat (GitHub/Telegram coordination the event stream
misses) is the honest bound on trace observability
