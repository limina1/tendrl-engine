#+TITLE: Discussion authoring — decision worksheet
#+TODO: DECIDE(d) LEAN(l) | DECIDED(D) DROPPED(x)

Companion to =docs/discussions-authoring-spec.md=. That spec reads as if
everything is settled; it isn't. This worksheet pulls out every place
where I made a judgment call or where a real choice still exists, and
unpacks each one: what the question is in plain terms, what prior art
says (Alexandria, the NIPs, our own code), what the options are, and
what I'd pick and why.

How to use it:
- =DECIDE= :: genuinely open — I have a lean but the call is yours.
- =LEAN= :: the spec already picked this; listed so you can veto. If you
  do nothing, the spec's choice stands.
- Flip items to =DECIDED= and fill in the =Decision:= line as you go.
  Once section A and B are all =DECIDED=, engine implementation (steps
  1–3 of the spec's build order) can start with zero ambiguity.

* A. Protocol / wire format
These decisions end up inside signed events on public relays. They are
the ones that are painful to change later — other clients will read
these tags forever. Everything else in this worksheet is our own code
and can be revised freely; section A deserves the most care.

** DECIDED A1: A comment on a section scopes to the section, not the book
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: protocol
:END:
*** What this is
When someone comments on section 3 of a publication, the comment event
carries a "root scope" — the uppercase =A= tag saying what the whole
thread is about. It can point at the section (=30041:pk:d-of-section=)
or at the whole publication (=30040:pk:d-of-book=). This determines
where the comment shows up when *other* clients fetch discussions, and
which address we must query to find it again.
*** Relevant patterns
- Alexandria roots at the *section* address; only comments typed in its
  publication-level box root at the 30040. Interop with existing
  Alexandria threads therefore favors section-rooting.
- NIP-22 just says root = "the event being commented on", which is the
  section — the user commented on that text, not on the table of
  contents.
- Our read side already queries per-section addresses
  (=discussions/counts= takes the address list), so section-rooted
  comments light up the right badges with no extra work.
*** Options
1. Root at the section; the publication-level comment box roots at the
   30040. Two scopes, each meaning what it says. ← spec's choice
2. Always root at the publication, tag the section some other way
   (e.g. a second lowercase =a=). Makes "fetch all discussion for this
   book" a single-address query, but violates least surprise and
   diverges from Alexandria.
*** My lean
Option 1. The single-query benefit of option 2 is already handled by
sending the full address list in one request, and the engine flattens
it. Nothing gained, interop lost.
*** Decision:
Option 1

** DECIDED A2: Write strict NIP-22 tags; stay lenient when reading
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: protocol
:END:
*** What this is
Alexandria's reply events contain two spec violations: a malformed
parent coordinate =["a", "1111:<pubkey>:"]= (kind 1111 events don't
*have* coordinates — they're not addressable) and a 4th element (the
author pubkey) stuffed into the =A= tag where NIP-22 doesn't define
one. Do we replicate those for compatibility, or emit clean tags?
*** Relevant patterns
- Our own =build_thread= (src/discussions.rs) threads purely off the
  lowercase =e= chain and ignores both broken tags — so *reading*
  Alexandria events already works and will keep working.
- Alexandria's own reader also threads off =e=, so it will read our
  clean events fine in the other direction.
*** Options
1. Strict on write, lenient on read (Postel's law). ← spec's choice
2. Byte-for-byte Alexandria compatibility. Propagates known bugs into
   the permanent record for no functional gain.
*** My lean
Option 1, firmly. There is no client whose threading depends on the
malformed tags.
*** Decision:
Option 1

** DECIDED A3: The =offset= tag on highlights — ship it, and in what form?
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: protocol
:END:
*** What this is
NIP-84 highlights carry the selected text but *no position*. If the
phrase "the state" appears four times in a section, nobody — including
us — can know which occurrence was highlighted. The spec proposes a
tendrl extension: =["offset", "<start>", "<end>"]=, character positions
(UTF-16 units, the unit JavaScript slices in) into the section's
content, with the highlight's =e= tag pinning *which version* of the
section those offsets refer to. Readers verify: if slicing the current
content at those offsets reproduces the highlighted text, trust the
offset; if not (the section was edited since), fall back to text
search. Foreign clients that don't know the tag lose nothing.
*** Relevant patterns
- Alexandria's *read* path honors an =offset= tag (it appears in their
  mock data) but their *write* path never emits one — so there's a
  soft precedent for the tag name, but no real ecosystem convention.
- W3C Web Annotation selectors (XPath/quote selectors) are the
  heavyweight alternative; they anchor to rendered DOM, which differs
  per client and per content format (Markdown vs AsciiDoc vs Org), so
  they're wrong for Nostr where the shared artifact is the raw event
  content string.
- Our resolver (=resolve_highlight_spans=) already works in UTF-16
  offsets, so the extension slots straight in.
*** Options
1. Ship =offset= + version-pin + verify-then-fallback. Duplicate
   phrases resolve correctly for tendrl-authored highlights; costs one
   tag. ← spec's choice
2. No offsets — pure substring matching like Alexandria. Simplest;
   wrong occurrence highlighted whenever text repeats.
3. Offsets *plus* a =context= tag always (not just optionally), so
   even foreign readers can disambiguate by neighborhood text.
   Slightly bigger events; helps clients that do fuzzy matching.
*** My lean
Option 1, and fold in option 3's habit cheaply: always send =context=
when the selection sits inside a paragraph (we have the text anyway).
Belt and suspenders, and =context= is standard NIP-84.
*** Decision:
option 1

** DECIDED A4: Non-standard =k= tag on highlight events
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: protocol
:END:
*** What this is
NIP-84 doesn't define a =k= tag on 9802 events. Adding =["k","30041"]=
(the kind of the highlighted event) lets relays and clients filter
"highlights of publications" vs "highlights of articles" in one query.
Some wild clients do this; it's not spec.
*** Options
1. Include it. Harmless one-liner, marginally useful. ← spec's choice
2. Omit; strictly NIP-84.
*** My lean
Include. Cost is zero, and our own future queries may want it.
*** Decision:
Include

** DECIDED A5: What happens when someone comments on a kind-1 note
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: protocol
:END:
*** What this is
NIP-22 explicitly forbids using 1111 to reply to kind-1 short notes —
those keep the older NIP-10 convention (a kind-1 event with marked =e=
tags). Tendrl barely surfaces kind 1 today (search rows, thread roots),
but the universal "Comment" action in EventViewModal will eventually
meet one. The endpoint has to do *something*.
*** Relevant patterns
- Alexandria's generic path does exactly this fork:
  =kind = parent.kind === 1 ? 1 : 1111= (CommentBox.svelte:185).
- Emitting 1111 against kind 1 anyway (some clients do) splits the
  thread: NIP-10 readers won't see it.
*** Options
1. The endpoint transparently builds a kind-1 NIP-10 reply instead.
   Caller doesn't branch; response's =event.kind= says what happened.
   ← spec's choice
2. Return 400 "kind 1 not supported" — honest, but turns a working
   button into an error for no user benefit.
3. Emit 1111 regardless — spec-violating, splits threads. No.
*** My lean
Option 1. It's ~30 lines (NIP-10 tags are simple) and makes the
affordance genuinely universal.
*** Decision:
Nostr is moving to kind 1111 for all comments

Resolution as folded into the spec: kind-1 roots also get a 1111
(=E=/=K=1/=P= scope) — the NIP-10 fork is dropped entirely, following
ecosystem direction over the vendored NIP-22's "MUST NOT". Caveat
recorded in the spec: NIP-10-only clients won't thread these replies
under the note; that cost shrinks as the ecosystem converges.

** DECIDED A6: External-id comments (DOI / URL / ISBN) — how much in v1?
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: protocol
:END:
*** What this is
NIP-22 + NIP-73 allow comments scoped to things that aren't Nostr
events at all: a paper's DOI, a web URL, a book's ISBN, a podcast
episode. The event carries =["I", "doi:10.1038/..."]["K", "doi"]=
instead of event pointers. For a reference manager this is quietly the
most on-mission form of commenting — discussion attached to *the
paper*, portable across all clients, independent of whether anyone
published the paper as a Nostr event. The catch: normalization. Two
clients writing the "same" DOI differently (case, hyphens, URL form)
create two disjoint threads, so the id munging must be engine-side and
exactly per NIP-73 (URLs stripped of fragments/trackers, ISBNs without
hyphens, DOIs lowercase).
*** Options
1. Endpoint support in v1 (one routing row + a normalization helper),
   UI surface later when refs/import panels want it. ← spec's choice
2. Defer entirely; keep the enum slot in the API shape so it's
   non-breaking to add.
3. Also build a v1 UI surface (e.g. "discuss" on knowledgebase docs
   that have a DOI).
*** My lean
Option 1. The engine work is small and forces the normalization
helper to exist early; UI can trail. Option 3 is tempting but expands
v1 — better as the first follow-up, since knowledgebase documents
already know their source URLs.
*** Decision:
Option 1

** DECIDED A7: Replying to a highlight — which event is the "root"?
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: protocol
:END:
*** What this is
This is the subtlest one, and I'd revise the spec depending on your
call. When a user replies to someone's highlight (in
DiscussionViewBuffer), the reply's uppercase root tags can point at:
(a) the *highlight* (=E= tag at the 9802 event — "this thread is about
the annotation"), or (b) the *section* (=A= tag at the 30041, with the
lowercase parent tags pointing at the 9802 — "this thread is about the
section, and this message specifically answers the highlight").
Both are legal NIP-22. They differ in *where the thread is visible*:
*** Relevant patterns
- Alexandria fetches 9802s *together with* 1111s under the same
  section-address filter and renders highlights as thread nodes among
  comments — implicitly the (b) worldview.
- Our own read side does the same: =discussions/list= defaults to
  kinds =[1111, 9802]= per address, and the reader shows both.
- The spec as written picked (a), on the plain reading of NIP-22
  ("root = the thing being commented on").
*** Options
1. Root at the 9802 (=E=/=K=9802/=P=). Clean semantics; but the reply
   carries *no section address at all*, so it is invisible to every
   section-scoped query — it won't appear in the reader's per-section
   thread, won't count in the =c N= badge, and only shows when someone
   opens that specific highlight. ← currently in the spec
2. Root at the section (=A= at the 30041), parent at the 9802
   (lowercase =e/k/p=). The reply shows up in the section's thread
   *and* threads correctly under the highlight; one fetch gets
   everything. Slight semantic stretch: the root scope names an event
   the replier may never have read.
*** My lean
Option 2, overriding the current spec text. Our whole read pipeline is
organized around section addresses; option 1 creates orphan
discussions our own surfaces would systematically miss. The stretch in
option 2 is the same one Alexandria and our reader already make by
treating highlights as section-thread citizens. If you agree I'll
amend spec §3.2.
*** Decision:

** DECIDED A8: Quote highlights (annotation inside the 9802) in v1
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: protocol
:END:
*** What this is
NIP-84 lets a highlight carry a =["comment", "<annotation>"]= tag —
"highlight this AND say something about it" as *one* event, rendered
like a quote-repost. The alternative (highlight event + separate 1111)
double-posts. UI cost is one optional text input on the highlight
popover.
*** Relevant patterns
Alexandria supports it (optional comment box in
HighlightSelectionHandler); NIP-84 defines it precisely to avoid the
two-event mess.
*** My lean
Include in v1 — it's an optional request field and an optional input.
Skip only the NIP-84 fine print about =mention=-marked p/r tags inside
the annotation (we don't parse annotation text in v1).
*** Decision:
Include in v1

* B. Engine / API design
Our own code — changeable later, but these shape the first PR.

** DECIDED B1: How the reply endpoint learns about the parent comment
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: engine
:END:
*** What this is
To build a reply, the engine needs two things from the parent comment:
its identity (id, kind, pubkey — for the lowercase parent tags) and
its *uppercase root tags* (to copy verbatim as the reply's root scope —
the "root chase"). The parent is usually in nostrdb because the user
is looking at it. Usually — not always: threads fetched from a relay
moments ago are ingested, but a comment rendered from an optimistic
refresh or an edge case might not be findable at sign time.
*** Options
1. Web sends only =parent.event_id=; engine loads it from nostrdb,
   404s if missing. Cleanest contract; occasional spurious failures.
2. Web always sends the full parent event JSON in the request; engine
   uses it directly. Never fails; but the engine builds root tags from
   client-supplied data without checking its own copy (fine — it's the
   user's own client, not a trust boundary; the user signs the result).
3. Hybrid: send id + full event; engine prefers its nostrdb copy,
   falls back to the supplied one. Most robust, slightly more code.
*** My lean
Option 3. The fallback path is ~5 lines and eliminates the one failure
mode users would actually hit ("Reply failed: event not found" on a
comment they are literally reading).
*** Decision:
Option 3

** DECIDED B2: What goes in the relay-hint slots of the tags
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: engine
:END:
*** What this is
NIP-22 tags have a third position for a relay URL hint — "where can a
stranger fetch the referenced event." It's optional and purely
advisory. The correct value is a relay where the *target* is actually
readable — i.e. one of our general/fetch relays, not our publish set.
*** Relevant patterns
- Alexandria passes a hint inconsistently (sometimes empty string).
- Our engine has runtime relay sets in =relay_store.rs=
  (general/fetch/publish) but does not track per-event "seen on relay
  X" provenance today.
*** Options
1. First configured =general= relay, empty string if none. One line,
   usually right.
2. Empty string everywhere in v1. Honest about not knowing; slightly
   less helpful to foreign clients.
3. Track per-event source relays at ingest and use the real one.
   Correct, but a new engine feature for a hint field — out of
   proportion.
*** My lean
Option 1. Revisit toward option 3 only if per-event provenance gets
built for some other reason.
*** Decision:
Option 1

** DECIDED B3: Delete is =POST /discussions/delete=, not HTTP DELETE
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: engine
:END:
*** What this is
Bike-shed but must be picked: the deletion endpoint's shape. It isn't
really a delete — it *creates* a kind-5 deletion-request event (sign +
ingest + broadcast, exactly like posting). HTTP DELETE with a request
body is also awkward in some clients.
*** My lean
=POST /api/v1/discussions/delete { event_id, reason }= — it's an event
publish; POST matches every other mutating route in =main.rs=. (The
spec at one point sketched =DELETE /discussions/:event_id=; treat this
worksheet as the fix.)
*** Decision:
Go with your lean

** DECIDED B4: How deletions are honored on the read side
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: engine
:END:
*** What this is
Publishing a kind-5 is the easy half. The other half: when
=discussions/list= serves comments, ones the author deleted should be
gone. Where does that filtering happen? nostrdb has its own NIP-09
handling (see =docs/nostrdb-deletion-architecture.org=) — it may
already hide deleted events from queries, or may not, or may only for
some query paths. I have not verified which.
*** Options
1. Rely on nostrdb's native NIP-09 handling if verification shows it
   covers our query path. Zero code.
2. Manual tombstone filter in =discussions_list_handler=: collect
   kind-5s whose =e= tags name events in the result set, drop matches
   where the kind-5 pubkey equals the target pubkey. ~20 lines, works
   regardless of nostrdb behavior.
3. Both (nostrdb native + manual belt-and-suspenders).
*** My lean
Read =nostrdb-deletion-architecture.org= as the first task of the
deletion step; do option 2 unless that doc proves option 1 suffices.
Correctness here is user-facing trust ("I deleted it and it's still
there" is a bad bug class).
*** Decision:
Nostrdb does not use delete on events, but we can emit deletion events and broadcast them/send them to a specific set of relays. I guess this should be a general option within the menu - Actions- (d) delete

Resolution: manual tombstone filter in =discussions_list_handler=
(option 2 — since nostrdb never physically removes events, the read
path must hide them itself), and deletion surfaces as a universal
Actions → =(d) delete= chord in EventViewModal for own events, with
the existing =relays= override for targeted broadcast.

** DECIDED B5: Do authored comments get "local / not yet accepted" status?
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: engine
:END:
*** What this is
Publications have =LocalPublicationTracker=: a signed event is marked
"local" until some relay accepts it, and the feed shows a pill so you
know it hasn't propagated. Comments/highlights could get the same
treatment — or not. Without it: the post response includes per-relay
accepted/rejected results once, the UI can toast a warning if 0/N
accepted, and that's it; the event lives in nostrdb either way and
can be re-broadcast manually via the existing broadcast endpoint.
*** Options
1. No persistent tracking in v1; per-relay results in the response +
   a toast on total failure. ← spec's choice
2. Extend =LocalPublicationTracker= (or generalize it) to arbitrary
   event kinds; render a "local" pill on unaccepted comments.
3. Generalize later, driven by an actual user story (offline writing
   sessions would motivate it).
*** My lean
Option 1 now, option 3 standing. Comments are small and cheap to
re-post; publications earned the tracker because they're multi-event
and expensive. Offline-first authoring is a real future story but
deserves its own design pass.
*** Decision:
option 1

** DECIDED B6: Input validation bounds
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: engine
:END:
*** What this is
What the endpoints reject. Not controversial, just needs stating:
- content: trimmed, non-empty; cap at 64 KB (relays commonly cap whole
  events around 64–512 KB; a comment near that is pathological).
- comments are plaintext per NIP-22 — but we do NOT try to strip or
  reject markdown syntax; we just render as plain text. Enforcement by
  rendering, not by parsing.
- highlight =content= must be non-empty and, when =offset= is given,
  must equal the slice of the pinned section content (cheap server-side
  sanity check that catches client offset bugs at write time, before
  they hit the permanent record).
*** My lean
As stated. The offset-must-match-slice write check is the one I'd
insist on: it makes corrupt offsets impossible rather than merely
handled.
*** Decision:
As stated

** DECIDED B7: Fix =discussions/counts= to see replies (bug, not a choice)
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: engine
:END:
*** What this is
Verified bug, listed for visibility rather than debate: replies carry
their root address only in the uppercase =A= tag, and
=discussion_counts_handler= filters on lowercase =#a= alone
(=api.rs:3189-3192=) — so every reply is invisible to the badge
counts. =discussions/list= already queries the =#a= ∪ =#A= ∪ =#e= trio
(=api.rs:3538-3564=); counts just needs the same filters plus dedup
before tallying (the two filters overlap on top-level comments, which
carry both tags).
*** My lean
Ship first, standalone. It improves current behavior before any
authoring code exists.
*** Decision:
Ship

* C. Web UI design
** DECIDED C1: After posting, refetch locally — don't splice state in TS
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: web
:END:
*** What this is
When a comment is posted, how does it appear on screen? Two idioms:
(a) optimistically insert the returned event into the local thread
state (instant, but requires re-implementing thread-tree insertion in
TypeScript — recreating the exact twin we deleted when threading moved
to Rust); (b) refetch =discussions/list {policy: local_only, threaded}=
— the engine ingested the event *before* responding, so the refetch
includes it, costs one local nostrdb read (sub-50ms), and threading
stays Rust-only.
*** Relevant patterns
Alexandria does client-side insertion plus a =setTimeout(refresh,
3000)= to catch relay echo — the hack exists because they have no
local-first store. We do; the whole point of ingest-before-respond is
that "refetch" is instant and authoritative.
*** My lean
(b), firmly — it's the frontend/backend boundary rule paying rent.
*** Decision:
B

** DECIDED C2: How selection maps to character offsets (the wrapping question)
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: web
:END:
*** What this is
To emit =offset= tags, the browser selection (DOM nodes + offsets)
must be converted to positions in the section's raw content string.
Complication: =RichContent= doesn't render the raw string — nostrdown
references render as chips/cards whose visible text differs from their
source text, so "count characters through the DOM" gives wrong answers
whenever a section contains a ={{ref}}= or wikilink. The renderer
already slices content into ordered segments, each of which *knows*
its source range internally; the question is how to expose that to the
selection-capture code.
*** Options
1. Emit =srcStart/srcEnd= on each segment and wrap text segments in
   =<span data-src-start>= (currently they render as bare text nodes).
   Selection endpoint → nearest wrapper → =srcStart= + offset inside
   it. Exact, simple lookup. Cost: extra spans in the reader's hot
   path — needs one visual/perf sanity check in a =<pre>= context, but
   spans are display-inline and style-free, so no change is expected.
   ← spec's choice
2. No DOM changes: walk the =<pre>='s child nodes in parallel with the
   segments array, matching by order. Zero DOM cost, but fragile —
   it depends on Svelte's DOM output mirroring the segment array
   one-to-one, which is an implementation detail that can silently
   change (adjacent text nodes merged, comment markers inserted).
3. Ignore the problem: use =selection.toString()= and search for it in
   the content string. Re-introduces the duplicate-phrase ambiguity
   that offsets exist to solve — circular.
*** My lean
Option 1. Option 2's failure mode is silent wrong offsets, which the
write-time slice check (B6) would turn into hard errors — better to
not generate them at all.
*** Decision:
Unsure about this one actually

Resolved empirically (2026-07-09): option 1 shipped and verified in
the running app — the wrapper spans changed nothing visually
(pre-wrap, style-free inline), and highlighting the SECOND occurrence
of a deliberately duplicated phrase produced =["offset","148","171"]=
on the wire with the overlay rendering on that occurrence only —
exactly the failure substring matching can't avoid. The end-to-end run
also exposed and fixed a pre-existing =buildSegments= bug (highlight
spans were silently dropped on sections without nostrdown refs).

** DECIDED C3: The highlight-creation gesture
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: web
:END:
*** What this is
How a reader actually creates a highlight, physically.
*** Relevant patterns
- Alexandria: an explicit "highlight mode" toggle button; while on,
  any completed selection immediately becomes a highlight (plus a
  preview/confirm dialog). Modal, but unambiguous.
- The modern reading-app idiom (Medium, Readwise, Hypothesis): select
  text → small floating popover appears near the selection with
  Highlight / Annotate actions.
- Tendrl is keyboard-centric: the WM has chords, buffers have =j/k/m=
  navigation. A selection + keystroke flow fits the app's character.
*** Options
1. Floating popover on selection-settle (debounced ~200ms), with
   Highlight and Annotate buttons. Discoverable; medium effort
   (positioning, dismissal rules, not firing in toolbars). ← spec
2. Mode toggle à la Alexandria. Simplest; but modal state the user
   must remember to turn off, and it fights normal copy-selection.
3. Keyboard only: select text, press =h= (highlight) / =H= or =c=
   (with annotation). Cheapest to build, invisible to new users.
4. Popover *and* keyboard chord driving the same action (popover for
   discoverability, chord for regulars).
*** My lean
Option 4, built as option 3 first (the action + offset math with a
keybinding is the testable core; the popover is presentation added
after). If effort must be cut, option 3 alone is an acceptable v1 in
this app's culture.
*** Decision:
I like 2 with a function for highlight mode that we can call with Spc :, but we can have a pill on the modeline here that calls up highlight mode for any event that we want to highlight. It is kind of like 2 but you press the button, select text and then bring up the popup like alexandria

Resolution: explicit *highlight mode*, entered two ways — a command
invocable from =Spc := and a modeline pill on highlightable buffers.
While the mode is on, completing a selection opens an Alexandria-style
confirm popover (selected-text preview + optional annotation input +
post/cancel); leaving the mode restores normal selection behavior.
Folded into spec §5.1.

** DECIDED C4: Where the universal "Comment" composer lives in EventViewModal
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: web
:END:
*** What this is
EventViewModal (the any-kind event inspector) gets a "Comment" action —
the entry point that makes commenting work across all of Nostr, not
just our reading surfaces. When pressed, where does typing happen?
*** Options
1. Inline: the action expands a textarea inside the modal's action
   area; post → toast + optional "view thread" link. Matches how the
   modal's other chord actions behave; lowest friction. ← my lean
2. Open a DiscussionView-style buffer pre-focused on a composer.
   More WM-idiomatic, heavier; a buffer for a one-line comment feels
   like ceremony.
3. Dedicated small compose modal stacked on the event modal.
   Modal-on-modal — avoid.
*** My lean
Option 1. If longer-form commenting on arbitrary events becomes a real
pattern, graduate to option 2 later (the old plan flags the same
"inline first, graduate to buffer if unhappy" principle for reply
boxes).
*** Decision:
The ultimate fallback for a universal comment option (like in publication, articles specs etc is to use the menu - Actions - (c) comment), but for commenting on a 1111, or any other event that is viewable, it should look like a social reply box and thread

Resolution: two tiers. (1) Universal fallback: EventViewModal Actions
→ =(c) comment= works on any event of any kind — expands an inline
textarea in the modal. (2) Events with a viewable surface (a 1111 or
9802 in DiscussionView, docs/articles with comment sections) render a
social-style thread with a reply box, which is the primary experience;
the modal action is the escape hatch for kinds with no view. Folded
into spec §5.4.

** DECIDED C5: One =ReplyBox= component, mounted on every surface as-is
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: web
:END:
*** What this is
The same article can render in =ReaderBuffer= (from search/refs) or
=DocBuffer= (from profiles) — a known duplication. Do we consolidate
those surfaces before adding comment affordances, or mount the same
components in both?
*** My lean
Mount in both; don't couple this feature to a surface consolidation.
The cost of "both" is two mount points for the same
=CommentThread=/=ReplyBox= pair, which is exactly what components are
for. Consolidation is its own (worthwhile, separate) cleanup.
*** Decision:
Mount in both

** DECIDED C6: Reply box is a plain textarea in v1
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: web
:END:
*** What this is
NIP-22 comment content is plaintext, so no formatting toolbar is
*possible*, but mentions (=nostr:npub…= with =p= tags), event quotes
(=q= tags), and autocomplete are all legal extras. Alexandria's
generic comment form supports basic markup parsing and @-mentions.
*** My lean
Plain =<textarea>=, Ctrl/Cmd-Enter to post, nothing else in v1.
Mentions/quotes are a clean follow-up (they're additive tags; no wire
format changes). Every feature added here delays the killer feature
(highlight capture) behind it in the build order.
*** Decision:
You have this well mapped out

** DECIDED C7: What signed-out users see
:PROPERTIES:
:AREA: web
:END:
*** What this is
When no identity can sign (=identityCanSign= false): do compose
affordances vanish, or render disabled with a "sign in to comment"
hint?
*** Options
1. Reply/comment boxes: visible but disabled with the hint (teaches
   that the feature exists). Highlight popover: not shown at all
   (a popover appearing on every text selection just to say "you
   can't" is noise). ← mixed rule, my lean
2. Hide everything. Cleanest reading surface; feature is
   undiscoverable until sign-in.
3. Show everything, prompt sign-in on interaction. Most aggressive
   funnel; interrupts reading flow.
*** My lean
Option 1's mixed rule — persistent affordances teach, transient ones
shouldn't nag.
*** Decision:
Option 1
