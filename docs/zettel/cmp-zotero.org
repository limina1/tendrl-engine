#+TYPE: comparison
#+STATUS: current
#+DATE: 2026-07-05

The reference-manager incumbent. Load-bearing patterns:

- *Metadata at reading speed.* Browser translators scrape site metadata;
  identifiers resolve against registries (DOI→CrossRef, ISBN→WorldCat,
  PMID→PubMed); orphan PDFs are salvaged by scanning early pages for an
  identifier. File and metadata-record are separate objects, so metadata
  can be retrofitted. → [[file:idea-registry-metadata.org][registry resolution]]
- *Orthogonal organization axes*: collections are set-membership
  playlists (item in many; delete a collection, items survive), tags are
  a second portable axis, saved searches are live virtual collections,
  "related items" are manual bidirectional links. Hierarchy is a view,
  never the storage model. Tendrl analog: 30040 indexes are the
  collections axis, tags the second, search the third.
- *CSL*: citation data fully separated from formatting; 10k+ community
  styles interpret one data model. The separation (data vs style) is the
  part we adopt; the style engine is the part we export to
  (→ [[file:idea-citation-export.org][citation export]]).
- *Better BibTeX*: pinned deterministic human citekeys + continuously
  materialized =.bib= export as the integration contract with external
  toolchains.
- *Annotation extraction*: PDF highlights are database objects extractable
  into notes with page-linked backlinks — highlights-as-data.

Pain points instructive for us: chronic sync-conflict storms (mutable
records — our signed immutable events don't have this), translator
breakage treadmill, collections that don't survive export as well as
tags.

Where tendrl differs on purpose: our "items" are addressable signed
events on an open network; sharing a citation is publishing an event,
not syncing a proprietary library.
