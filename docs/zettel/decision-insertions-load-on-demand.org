#+TITLE: Decision: Insertions Load On Demand, Pointing At Their Anchor
#+TYPE: decision
#+STATUS: current
#+DATE: 2026-09-16

* The decision

Insertions and annotations are *never* loaded automatically with a
publication. A reader action — "load annotations" — fetches them (the
=#A= filter, Confirm-gated like any relay fetch) and each result is
rendered /pointing at/ the section it anchors to: a post-it stuck to a
page, visibly not a page. Promotion is a separate, explicit act: the
logged-in author copies the publication with the insertion spliced in
(see the spec's /Promotion/ section). (User's call, 2026-09-16.)

* Why

- *The index stays what it says.* Until the reader asks, a publication
  is exactly its 30040. The "attached vs in the structure" distinction
  the spec makes is only legible if the two never blend silently.
- *No author policy needed.* "Whose insertions?" was an open question
  because an automatic pass has to decide. An explicit load carries its
  author filter per request (own + assistant by default, editable), so
  there is no standing policy and no web-of-trust dependency.
- *Consistent with Confirm mode.* A load is a fetch; it goes through
  the same intent modal as every other relay read.
- *The metaphor is the affordance.* A post-it is something you notice
  stuck to the page, not something the page absorbed. Rendering the
  pointer is the UI stating the protocol fact.

* Consequences

- =load_insertions= is an endpoint behind a button, not a pass inside
  =stream_publication_tree= — and its input is a /spell/ (=A:<root>
  k:30041= plus the modal's author filter), so the collective table's
  joins ("3 of 14 authors", by section, by time) are the same mechanism
  with a different query, never a client-side filter over everything.
- The reader needs a pointing/attached visual for loaded insertions —
  a design session, not a retrofit ([[file:feat-ui-patterns.org][UI patterns]]).
- Closes the author-policy question in the plan.
