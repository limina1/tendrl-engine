#+TITLE: Comparison: folksonomy heritage (del.icio.us → Pinboard) & hashtag failure modes
#+TYPE: comparison
#+STATUS: current
#+DATE: 2026-07-05

The prior art for reader-side tagging, positive and negative.

- *del.icio.us* was the first mass post-hoc labeling system: the
  /bookmarker/, not the page author, tagged content; the URL was the
  join key ("who else saved this? what did they call it? follow their
  stream"). On Nostr the event id/naddr plays the URL's role.
- *Uncontrolled vocabularies converge.* The classic pathologies are real
  (synonyms, polysemy, plural chaos, no hierarchy) — but Golder &
  Huberman's finding on delicious data is the design key: per-object tag
  distributions stabilize after fewer than ~100 taggers, a power-law
  consensus with minority tags coexisting. So: don't gate labels on a
  controlled vocabulary; *aggregate and display the distribution*.
  Caveat carried forward: Nostr taggers are sybil-cheap, unlike
  delicious accounts — which is why labeler *choice* (whose labels count)
  is the load-bearing control, with WoT weighting deferred
  ([[file:scope-boundaries.org][scope]]).
- *Tag gardens*: folksonomies stay usable where renaming/merging one's
  own tags is cheap. A label browser needs bulk-rename from day one.
- *Hashtag discovery's failure modes (Fediverse)*: author-side,
  self-asserted, unranked, viewpoint-dependent (you see only what your
  instance federated), fragmenting vocabularies, spam at equal weight
  with expertise. Post-hoc labels from chosen readers fix each of these
  — and tendrl's explicit fetch intents at least make the
  viewpoint-partiality /visible/, which Mastodon never did.
- *Pinboard's survival lessons*: personal archive value must not depend
  on network effects (worth it at n=1); the reader's copy outlives the
  platform; boring, small, sustainable beats viral. Delicious died of
  acquisitions; Pinboard bought the corpse for $35k.
